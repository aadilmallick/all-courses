# Ghar ki Baat

## A thousand hours toward the conversations that matter

This course is for an English-speaking adult who wants to participate in a Hyderabadi family’s everyday conversations without learning Urdu or Devanagari writing. It assumes no prior Urdu or Hindi. All learner-facing language is in Roman script. You will learn to greet elders, describe your day, understand family relationships, tell stories, make plans, discuss a problem, express a view respectfully, and keep a conversation alive when words are missing.

The twelve chapters are a continuous textbook, not a phrase list. Each builds a grammatical relationship, shows how to assemble sentences, then puts the relationship into original conversations. Read the explanations, say the examples, use the exercises and their answer keys, and carry the chapter’s family mission into a real exchange. The companion website adds translation practice, grammar questions, listening transcription, voice recording, vocabulary review, and a local journal.

## What the thousand hours mean

The chapter allocations total exactly 1,000 hours. They include learning, repeated listening, retrieval, conversation, feedback, and reassessment. Reading this book once is not a thousand hours of learning. A useful rhythm is ten hours per week for one hundred weeks, or twenty hours per week for fifty weeks. Your pace can change; logged practice measures actual time rather than automatically awarding the advertised chapter allocation.

Spend the time producing and interpreting messages, rather than repeating an answer until you remember its place on the page. Return to the same grammar with new people, different objects, a fresh story, or an unexpected follow-up. Several hundred hours should involve real interaction or recorded conversation work. The browser cannot supply a relative’s reactions, local intonation, or a spontaneous interruption.

## What “Hyderabadi” means here

Shared conversational Urdu/Hindi gives you a broad grammatical foundation. Local Dakhni forms appear throughout, with explicit shared alternatives and register notes. A family might use **hau**, **nakko**, **kaiku**, or **mereku**, and still prefer shared forms in another setting. Dakhni has its own history and complex relationships with Urdu and Hindi; naming it is also a matter of identity. This course follows the learner’s requested description without trying to settle that identity question.

Follow the speech of people you know. Use **aap** with elders and unfamiliar adults until they indicate another preference. A local word is not a license to use a teasing tone with everyone. Keep an English-and-Roman notebook of expressions your family actually uses, their meaning, who said them, and an audio model where possible. This becomes the personal part of the course that no general textbook can write for you.

## Roman spelling and grammatical gender

The course uses readable Roman forms such as **main** for “I,” **mein** for “in,” **hoon**, **hai**, **hain**, **nahin**, and long vowels often written **aa**, **ee**, or **oo**. Roman Urdu has no single universal spelling. Some conventional spellings differ across chapters or family messages; listen for the spoken form, and consult the Roman guide. Exercises list selected alternatives but cannot enumerate every valid spelling or translation.

Examples identify masculine and feminine agreement where it changes a sentence. A grammatical gender describes a noun’s agreement class; it does not claim that an object has a biological gender. Speaker gender and respectful reference also affect forms. Learn a noun with a descriptive phrase and a verb in context. Ask a speaker which self-reference feels right for you when the textbook’s paired examples do not fit.

## The goal and its limits

The endpoint is conversation aligned with selected CEFR B2 spoken interaction and listening abilities: developing a view with reasons, narrating clearly, handling familiar complications, and interacting without a script. It is a learning target, not an accredited examination or a guarantee. A core of 1,000 useful words does not contain every word needed for B2. The course also teaches productive combinations, topic expansion, circumlocution, and how to keep learning from your family.

No recorded native-speaker audio is bundled. The site supports saved family recordings and synthetic Urdu/Hindi system voices where the device provides them. Synthetic speech is not a reliable model of a Hyderabadi accent. Machine-only native-script spelling may be passed to a voice internally; the learner never has to read it. Unsupported phrases fail with guidance rather than silently being read in an English voice. The speaking studio records and plays your voice; optional local recognition checks word sequences where a compatible language pack exists. Neither recognition nor the optional English-language AI reflection coach can certify pronunciation.

## Reading order

Read chapters 1–4 for the first everyday exchanges, chapters 5–8 to connect events and explanations, and chapters 9–12 to develop nuance and independence. Keep the grammar reference and Hyderabadi field guide nearby. At each milestone, use a fresh conversation and an unfamiliar listening sample, then decide what to revisit. Your most useful evidence is a conversation that becomes easier to share.
