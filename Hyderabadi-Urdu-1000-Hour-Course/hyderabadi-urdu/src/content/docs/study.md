## Start with a small exchange

Open chapter 1 and read its first conversation. Say each line, then use the English column to recover the Roman sentence without looking. Substitute your name and one relative’s name. Keep the first exchange short enough that you can say it while looking at the other person. A good beginner conversation includes a greeting, an answer, a question, and a repair phrase.

Read an explanation until you can account for the change between two examples. For **mera kamra** and **meri chai**, the relevant difference is the possessed noun’s gender, not the speaker’s gender. Write another pair. Say it without reading. Return to the worked example if you cannot explain your choice.

## A listening ladder

First listen for the situation: who is speaking, to whom, and why? On the second pass, listen for the action and the time. On the third, recover the exact phrase and compare the transcript. Shadow only a short segment, a little behind the speaker, then say it alone. Finally change a detail and have someone respond. Copying a model perfectly does not by itself show that you understood it.

Synthetic voices can help with selected shared Urdu/Hindi phrases where a supported device voice and machine speech spelling exist. They cannot teach all local rhythm. The strongest model is a willing relative’s natural recording. In the speaking studio, save the exact Roman transcript with the audio file. Keep the original audio as a backup. No native-speaker recordings ship with this edition.

## Practice modes

**Urdu → English** checks whether you recovered a message. **English → Urdu** asks you to produce a model frame. **Grammar** isolates one meaningful choice. **Listen & transcribe** hides the transcript until you answer and asks separately for the English meaning. **Speak aloud** supplies a model and a self-check; use the recording studio to compare takes. **Vocabulary review** schedules selected cards after ten minutes, one day, or longer intervals according to your recall rating.

Answer matching ignores punctuation and capitalization but preserves distinctions such as **main/ mein** and **kal/kaal**. It compares accepted strings rather than understanding every possible paraphrase. When your answer is natural but unlisted, compare the grammar and meaning with the explanation or ask a speaker. Do not train yourself to mistrust valid language merely because an exact-match quiz rejected it.

## Selection audio

Select a Roman word or sentence in a lesson and right-click to open **Hear selection**. Saved audio for that exact transcript takes priority; otherwise the site looks for a supported Urdu/Hindi system voice and mapped speech input. Hold Shift while right-clicking for the browser’s ordinary menu. The lesson’s audio feature does not change the meaning or content of your selection.

If the site reports no voice, install an Urdu or Hindi system voice and restart the browser, or upload a family clip. If it reports an unmapped phrase, save a clip for that exact selection. Approximate Roman TTS is an explicit audio setting; it may pronounce Roman spelling incorrectly and should not be your native model.

## Speak without making every turn an exam

Before a call, pick one purpose and two structures: explain tomorrow’s plan, ask for a preference, or retell a small incident. Let the conversation happen. Afterwards, record three things you understood, two things you could not say, and one expression you want to reuse. Ask for one correction at a time. Constant interruption for every ending makes a meaningful conversation hard for everyone.

Practice takes in the browser are temporary until downloaded. Uploaded family/reference clips persist in local browser storage. Journal backups contain practice history, hours and notes; they do not contain audio. Save originals outside the browser. Optional recognition requests local processing only; when a pack is unsupported, it reports the limitation instead of silently switching to a remote recognizer.

## Keep the language personal and broad

The examples use families, food, visits, work and everyday life. Add your own interests and your household’s names for things. Use words outside the 1,000-entry core when you need them. Ask **Isko aap kya kehte hain?** and repeat the answer in a complete sentence. The course’s most useful ending is the point where you can keep learning from real conversation.
