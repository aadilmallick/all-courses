# Roman letters, real sounds

## A reading aid, not English spelling

You never have to read Urdu or Devanagari script in this course. Roman letters point to sounds; they do not mean that a word should be pronounced like a similarly spelled English word. Ask a speaker to model a whole phrase and compare the rhythm. There is no universally enforced Roman Urdu spelling, so variation is expected.

| Course cue | What to listen for | Useful model |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a / aa | a shorter central vowel versus a longer open vowel | kal / naam |
| i / ee | short versus long high front vowel | din / theek |
| u / oo | short versus long high rounded vowel | tum / door |
| e / ai | vowel qualities often written inconsistently in messages | mera / hai |
| o / au | distinct rounded vowels; English spellings can mislead | do / kaun |
| bh, dh, gh, jh | voiced consonant with an accompanying breathy quality | bhai / ghar |
| ph, th, kh, chh | aspiration, a release of air; not English “f” or “th” by default | phal / theek / khana / chhota |
| sh / s | two distinguishable sounds | shukriya / saaf |
| q, kh, gh, z | sounds retained to varying degrees in Urdu and regional speech | qeemat / khair / ghair / zaroor |

Some conventional forms retain familiar spellings rather than a strict one-symbol-one-sound system. **Main** means “I,” with a nasalized vowel; **mein** means “in.” Their vowel qualities differ, and nasalization is not an extra strong English “n.” **Hain** also contains a nasalized vowel. A relative may write **mai**, **main**, **me**, or **mein** in messages; use context while keeping the course distinction in your own answers.

## Dental and retroflex sounds without a new alphabet

For a dental **t/d**, place the tongue near the upper teeth. For a retroflex **t/d**, bring the tongue tip farther back toward the roof of the mouth. English “t” and “d” are not exact models of either set. Ask for contrasting words and a slowed phrase. The course usually uses ordinary **t/d** rather than technical symbols; chapter 1 explains the sound difference, and the recording matters more than the typography.

Do not confuse **th** with the English sound in “thin.” In a word like **theek**, it cues an aspirated stop. Place a hand in front of your mouth and compare an unaspirated and an aspirated version with a speaker. Do not add a full separate “huh” syllable.

## A three-minute routine

Listen to **Aap kaise hain?**, then say only **aap**, then **kaise**, then **hain**. Reassemble the phrase without equal pauses between every word. Record it. Compare vowel length and the transition between words. Next say the same phrase with a warm greeting tone and with a genuine question tone. Intonation conveys social meaning that a spelling table cannot encode.

When you find a family spelling different from the book, enter both in your notebook with the same audio reference. Quizzes accept selected spelling alternatives, but their list is finite. The aim is intelligible speech and comprehension, not enforcing one Roman spelling tradition.
