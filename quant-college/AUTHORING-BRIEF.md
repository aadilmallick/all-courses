# Quantitative Finance from First Principles

Audience: an adult beginner; no finance, programming, calculus, or university mathematics assumed. Arithmetic is rebuilt before symbolic work. Goal: independent entry-level quantitative research and preparation to study all eight named university courses, with measurable project evidence. Completion is not an employment guarantee.

Format: 18 substantial chapters, a continuous Markdown volume, an offline HTML reader with typeset mathematics, an interactive local browser laboratory, complete Python examples, source ledger, downloadable resource index, a course coverage matrix, worked project guidance, and a portable ZIP. Canonical text lives in chapters. No source books or course videos are republished.

Sequence: numeracy and reasoning; calculus; linear algebra; Python; probability; statistics; valuation; portfolio and asset pricing; econometrics; univariate time series; multivariate time series and Bayesian computation; convex optimization; stochastic calculus; derivatives and simulation; dynamic choice and risk; machine learning; research workflow; integrated projects and readiness.

Conventions: returns are decimals; time is in years unless explicitly stated; vectors are columns; covariance is positive semidefinite; population parameters and estimates are distinguished; P denotes the physical probability model and Q a pricing measure; cash-flow timing and information availability are explicit. Synthetic examples never constitute market evidence.

Pacing: roughly 1,800–2,400 hours including the mapped source lectures, original lessons, course problem sets, programming, and projects. At 15–20 hours/week this is a multi-year program. The schedule is a planning estimate, not a validated learning-time measurement.

Research date: November 12, 2026, America/New_York. Official resource pages are inspected programmatically; linked downloads are inventoried separately from files actually opened. External availability can change. Historical MATLAB/R assignments remain references; Python examples are original translations of concepts, not claims of complete assignment-port equivalence.
