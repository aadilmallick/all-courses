# Chapter 10 — Hints and reference solutions

All products use right-to-left composition, as in [Chapter 10](../chapters/10-permutations-and-alternating-groups.md).

## 1. Cycle decomposition

**Hint.** Follow one unused input until it returns, then start another.

**Solution.** The paths are $1\mapsto3\mapsto4\mapsto1$ and $2\mapsto5\mapsto2$, giving $\sigma=(1\ 3\ 4)(2\ 5)$. The inverse is $(1\ 4\ 3)(2\ 5)$. Its order is $\operatorname{lcm}(3,2)=6$. Its sign is $(-1)^{3-1}(-1)^{2-1}=-1$, so it is odd. The inverse has the same order and sign.

## 2. Composition order

**Hint.** Compute every input through the rightmost factor first.

**Solution.** For $\alpha\beta$, the inputs move $1\mapsto4\mapsto1$ as a two-cycle, and $2\mapsto3\mapsto2$ as another; explicitly $1\to3\to4$, $4\to4\to1$, $2\to2\to3$, and $3\to1\to2$. Thus $\alpha\beta=(1\ 4)(2\ 3)$. For $\beta\alpha$, we get $1\to2\to2$, $2\to3\to1$, $3\to4\to4$, and $4\to1\to3$, hence $(1\ 2)(3\ 4)$. The factors do not commute. Both results are even, as expected from multiplying two odd permutations.

## 3. Powers of a cycle

**Hint.** Label cycle positions by residues modulo its length.

**Solution.** A power by $m$ adds $m$ to a position modulo $k$. The subgroup generated by $[m]$ in $\mathbb Z/k\mathbb Z$ has size $k/\gcd(k,m)$. Its cosets are the cycles of the power, so there are $\gcd(k,m)$ of them, each of that size. For $k=8,m=6$, there are two cycles of length four. Tracing gives $(1\ 7\ 5\ 3)(2\ 8\ 6\ 4)$.

## 4. Orders in A_5

**Hint.** Use the disjoint cycle lengths and their signs.

**Solution.** A five-cycle is even and has order five, a three-cycle is even and has order three, and $(1\ 2)(3\ 4)$ is even and has order two. An order-six permutation on five symbols would need disjoint cycle lengths three and two, since no single cycle can have length six. But a three-cycle is even and a two-cycle odd, making their product odd. Thus no element of $A_5$ has order six.

## 5. Transpositions and normal subgroups

**Hint.** Conjugation relabels the two exchanged symbols.

**Solution.** Given transpositions $(a\ b)$ and $(c\ d)$, choose a permutation $\sigma$ with $\sigma(a)=c$ and $\sigma(b)=d$, extending the assignment bijectively to the remaining symbols. Then $\sigma(a\ b)\sigma^{-1}=(c\ d)$. If a normal subgroup contains one transposition, it contains all its conjugates, hence every transposition. Since transpositions generate $S_n$, it equals $S_n$. For $n=1$ there are no transpositions, so the conditional statement has no instance.

## 6. Three-cycle plus two-cycle

**Hint.** Choose the symbols of the three-cycle, then choose one of its two directions.

**Solution.** There are $\binom53\cdot2=20$ such permutations. The remaining two symbols form the unique transposition on that pair. Each permutation has order $\operatorname{lcm}(3,2)=6$ and sign $(-1)^2(-1)^1=-1$. Thus all twenty lie outside $A_5$.

## 7. Conjugating double transpositions in A_4

**Hint.** Relabel by the even permutation $(1\ 2\ 3)$.

**Solution.** Write $v_1=(1\ 2)(3\ 4)$, $v_2=(1\ 3)(2\ 4)$, and $v_3=(1\ 4)(2\ 3)$. Conjugation by $c=(1\ 2\ 3)$ sends $v_1$ to $(2\ 3)(1\ 4)=v_3$, sends $v_3$ to $(2\ 4)(3\ 1)=v_2$, and sends $v_2$ to $v_1$. Thus they are conjugate within $A_4$. Every element of order two in $A_4$ is one of these double transpositions. A subgroup of order two contains exactly one of them and the identity, so conjugation carries its nonidentity element outside the subgroup. Hence none of those subgroups is normal.

## 8. Cayley representation of the four-element product group

**Hint.** Label the elements $0,a,b,a+b$ by $1,2,3,4$.

**Solution.** Addition by $a$ exchanges $0$ with $a$ and $b$ with $a+b$, giving $(1\ 2)(3\ 4)$. Addition by $b$ gives $(1\ 3)(2\ 4)$, and addition by $a+b$ gives $(1\ 4)(2\ 3)$. The identity translation is $e$. The image is therefore $\{e,(1\ 2)(3\ 4),(1\ 3)(2\ 4),(1\ 4)(2\ 3)\}$, the subgroup $V$ already seen in $A_4$. Composition corresponds to adding the translating elements, so the map is a bijective homomorphism onto that subgroup.
