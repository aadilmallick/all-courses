# Chapter 25 — Hints and worked solutions

## 1. A fixed quadratic field

**Hint.** Use the basis $1,\sqrt2,\sqrt3,\sqrt6$.

The simultaneous sign change sends $a+b\sqrt2+c\sqrt3+d\sqrt6$ to $a-b\sqrt2-c\sqrt3+d\sqrt6$. Equality forces $2b=2c=0$, hence $b=c=0$ in characteristic zero. The fixed elements are exactly $a+d\sqrt6$, so the fixed field is $\mathbb Q(\sqrt6)$.

## 2. Orders and indices

**Hint.** Artin's theorem gives the top degree; the tower law gives the bottom one.

We have $[E:E^H]=|H|=4$. Since $[E:F]=|G|=24$, the tower law yields $[E^H:F]=24/4=6$. No normality of $H$ is needed for these degree formulas. If $H$ is not normal, the bottom extension need not be Galois.

## 3. Two different hypotheses

**Hint.** Separability concerns repeated roots; normality concerns whether all roots remain in the field.

Characteristic zero makes the irreducible polynomial $X^3-2$ separable, so the extension is separable. The field is real, while two of the polynomial's roots are nonreal, so it does not contain all roots of an irreducible polynomial having one root in it. Hence it is not normal. Its automorphism group is trivial, but that alone would not identify which Galois hypothesis failed without these two arguments.

## 4. A finite-field quotient

**Hint.** The fourth Frobenius power generates a subgroup of order three.

The full Galois group is cyclic of order twelve. The subgroup $\langle\sigma^4\rangle$ has order three and index four. Its fixed field is the unique degree-four extension of $\mathbb F_2$ inside the ambient field, hence $\mathbb F_{16}$. Since every subgroup of a cyclic group is normal, the fixed field is Galois over the prime field, with quotient group $C_{12}/\langle\sigma^4\rangle\cong C_4$. The restricted Frobenius generates this quotient action.

## 5. Conjugating a fixed field

**Hint.** Apply a conjugated automorphism to an element of the form $\sigma(x)$.

If $x\in E^H$ and $h\in H$, then $(\sigma h\sigma^{-1})(\sigma x)=\sigma h(x)=\sigma x$. Thus $\sigma(E^H)\subseteq E^{\sigma H\sigma^{-1}}$. Conversely, if $y$ is fixed by every $\sigma h\sigma^{-1}$, applying $\sigma^{-1}$ to the equality shows $h(\sigma^{-1}y)=\sigma^{-1}y$ for every $h$. Thus $\sigma^{-1}y\in E^H$, giving $y\in\sigma(E^H)$. Both containments prove equality.

## 6. Why a set of cosets is not automatically a quotient group

**Hint.** Complex conjugation fixes the real cube root.

The subgroup is the order-two group generated by complex conjugation, corresponding to a transposition in $S_3$. It is not normal because conjugation by a three-cycle produces a different transposition. Hence its cosets do not carry the well-defined group multiplication used for quotient groups. There is a three-element coset set, but no quotient group $S_3/C_2$ for this chosen subgroup. This agrees with the real cubic field not being Galois over $\mathbb Q$.

## 7. Which part of a tower stays Galois

**Hint.** The same splitting polynomials still work over the larger base field.

Write $E$ as a splitting field of finitely many polynomials over $F$. They are also polynomials over $K$, split in $E$, and their roots still generate $E$ over $K$, so $E/K$ is normal. The minimal polynomial of any element over $K$ divides a separable polynomial over $F$, so it is separable. Therefore $E/K$ is Galois. The assertion for $K/F$ fails in the real cubic intermediate field of the splitting field of $X^3-2$: that intermediate field omits two conjugates. Group-theoretically its fixing subgroup is not normal.

## 8. Descending coefficients to the fixed field

**Hint.** The assumed independence is over $K$, but the linear system is solved over $E$.

A nonzero solution alone gives an $E$-linear relation among the selected elements. That does not contradict their $K$-independence; elements independent over a smaller field are often dependent over a larger one. Choosing a solution of minimal support and normalizing one coefficient to one lets us compare it with every automorphism-transformed solution. The difference would have smaller support unless it vanished. Therefore all coefficients are fixed by the automorphism group and belong to $K$. Only then does the identity-row equation contradict the initial $K$-independence. The step is a descent argument, not merely a way to find some linear relation.
