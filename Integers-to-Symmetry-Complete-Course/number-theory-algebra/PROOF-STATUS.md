# Proof status and mathematical boundaries

The table records how the course treats major results. A full proof in a chapter means the argument is supplied there or built from earlier proved lemmas. It is not a claim that a formal proof assistant has certified it. Selected arguments were independently reviewed and numerical examples were checked; the verification report states the scope of that evidence.

| Result family | Chapters | Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logic, induction and well-ordering | 1 | Proof methods explained from elementary integer/set reasoning; standard foundational properties of the integers are assumed |
| Euclid, Bézout, Euclid’s lemma, unique integer factorization | 2–3 | Proved with worked intermediate calculations |
| Congruence operations, linear congruences, generalized CRT | 4–5 | Well-definition, existence, compatibility, counts and uniqueness proved |
| Subgroups, cosets, Lagrange, quotient groups and isomorphism | 6–8 | Main claims proved; normality checked before quotient operations |
| Fermat, Euler, Wilson, primitive roots, prime-power unit groups | 9 | Proved; local polynomial and binomial prerequisites supplied |
| Permutation parity, actions, Burnside, class equation | 10–11 | Proved and illustrated by finite examples |
| Cauchy and Sylow theorems | 12 | Full counting/action and induction proofs supplied |
| Ideals, quotient rings, prime/maximal criteria and ring CRT | 13–14 | Proved with convention and properness checks |
| Polynomial division, root bound and irreducibility methods | 15 | Proved in the relevant coefficient domains |
| Euclidean domains ⇒ PIDs ⇒ UFDs; Gaussian arithmetic | 16 | Proof chain and arithmetic applications supplied; converses not assumed |
| Euler’s criterion, Gauss’s lemma and quadratic reciprocity | 17–18 | Complete elementary arguments, including the reciprocity sign and supplementary laws |
| Möbius inversion and arithmetic-function identities | 19 | Finite divisor-sum and convolution proofs supplied |
| Continued fractions and Pell equations | 20 | Convergent identities and Pell existence/classification developed; full general periodicity of quadratic continued fractions is separately identified as a theorem used |
| Smith form and finite abelian classification | 21 | Constructive existence and intrinsic uniqueness supplied; the general gcd-of-minors formula is an optional stated extension |
| Extension degrees, tower law and construction obstructions | 22 | Linear algebra rebuilt locally; main claims proved |
| Algebraic integer closure and quadratic integer rings | 23 | Finite-spanning-module/determinant and trace/norm proofs supplied |
| General ideal factorization/class-group theory | 23 | Broader theory identified as further theorems, not fully developed or proved |
| Finite fields: existence, uniqueness, cyclic units and Frobenius | 24 | Constructive proofs supplied; trivial multiplicative group handled separately |
| Artin’s fixed-field theorem and finite Galois correspondence | 25 | Core lemmas, correspondence and normality criterion proved; finite setting and separability stated |
| Cyclotomic irreducibility and regular-polygon criterion | 26 | Full algebraic arguments supplied; standard Euclidean construction operations are explained geometrically |
| Concrete Galois groups and nonsolvability of A₅ | 27 | Concrete group calculations, modular certificates, and A₅ argument supplied |
| Reduction-cycle theorem and general radical-solvability equivalence | 27 | Precisely stated imported theorems; proof mechanisms sketched, with Milne theorem references; not labeled full proofs |
| Positive series, Euler product for s > 1 and prime reciprocal divergence | 28 | Elementary finite-to-limit arguments supplied; completeness of the real numbers is used as a foundational principle |
| Prime number theorem | 28 | Stated for orientation; not proved |
| Optional sharper integral bound | 28 | Identified calculus result; not silently assumed in earlier elementary proofs |
| Simple-root Hensel lifting and compatible p-adic residues | 29 | Algebraic lifting and inverse-limit arithmetic developed; the analytic completion viewpoint is distinguished from the construction used |
| Polynomial evaluation codes, interpolation, distance and erasures | 30 | Finite-field proofs and worked examples supplied; the companion demonstrates erasures rather than an unknown-error decoder |

The course provides a broad two-semester spine with substantial extensions. It does not equate a numerical experiment with an infinite theorem, and it does not claim to replace a full graduate course in analytic number theory, algebraic number theory, or general Galois theory.
