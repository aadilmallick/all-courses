# Independent mathematical review: Chapters 05, 08 and 15

Review scope: read-only review of the finished chapter files and their associated reference solutions, emphasizing generalized CRT, quotient well-definedness/isomorphisms and polynomial irreducibility/root bounds. This is a mathematical reading review, not a formal proof-assistant certification or a review of every course chapter.

## Findings

No substantive mathematical error was identified in the reviewed material.

- Chapter 05 correctly changes the modulus when canceling a gcd, handles the reduced modulus-one boundary explicitly, proves the generalized compatibility condition modulo gcd and uniqueness modulo lcm, distinguishes pairwise coprimality from an overall gcd condition, and explains prime-power root counts without applying prime-element arguments to arbitrary composites. The finite-list compatibility explanation via maximal prime powers is valid. The exercise solutions' CRT representatives and idempotents are consistent.
- Chapter 08 explicitly checks representative independence for normal-subgroup coset multiplication, uses the image rather than the entire target in the first isomorphism theorem, handles normality relative to the correct ambient group, and states the containment hypotheses for the correspondence and staged-quotient theorems. The square-group counterexample and the quotient by its center are correct under the order-eight convention. The solutions distinguish additive quotient groups from their subgroup kernels correctly.
- Chapter 15 distinguishes formal polynomials from polynomial functions, identifies where the integral-domain hypothesis is used in the root bound, proves irreducible implies prime using Bezout rather than circular unique-factorization reasoning, and checks irreducibility relative to the coefficient field. The reduction and Eisenstein arguments preserve positive degrees by checking leading coefficients. The quotient-field criterion, finite-field examples, and exercise solutions are mathematically consistent.

## Limits

This review does not establish learner mastery, universal rendering compatibility or that every theorem elsewhere in the course has been independently checked. General advanced results cited in later chapters retain their stated proof status.
