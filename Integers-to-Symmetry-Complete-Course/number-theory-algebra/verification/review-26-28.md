# Independent mathematical review — Chapters 26 and 28

Reviewer scope: the complete chapter and solution files for Chapters 26 and 28 were read independently after their author's revisions. This is a mathematical manuscript review, not a formal proof-assistant certification or a check of every platform's math renderer. No changes were made to another author's chapter files.

## Chapter 26

No substantive mathematical error found in the reviewed version. Checked the distinction between the cyclic root group and the unit-exponent Galois group; the root-order formula; divisor factorization and integrality of cyclotomic polynomials; the shifted Eisenstein argument; and the general reduction-modulo-an-auxiliary-prime irreducibility proof. In particular, the auxiliary prime is explicitly required not to divide the index, ensuring that the reduction of $X^n-1$ is squarefree. Monicity preserves nonzero degrees on reduction, so the common-factor contradiction works.

Checked the automorphism-to-exponent correspondence, the maximal real subfield degree, the pentagon coordinate equation, and the eighth-root fixed-field calculations. The real-coordinate discussion correctly warns that extracting the imaginary coordinate can require adjoining $i$; the regular hexagon example illustrates this boundary accurately. The regular-polygon sufficiency argument uses the real Galois subfield, an abelian group of power-of-two order, and a subgroup chain, rather than the invalid claim that arbitrary degree-four numbers must be constructible. The Fermat-prime factorization criterion and the six polygon decisions in the solutions agree with the totient values. All eight exercises' reference answers were reviewed.

Minor convention boundary, not a substantive error: the statement for every positive $n$ includes $n=1$, while the course generally uses residue-ring notation for moduli at least two. The usual trivial-group interpretation of the units modulo one makes the displayed Galois formula correct, or the formula may explicitly be restricted to $n\ge2$ with $K=\mathbb Q$ handled separately. No argument later in this chapter depends on the exceptional notation.

## Chapter 28

No substantive mathematical error found. Checked the epsilon definition, monotone-bounded argument, dyadic harmonic lower bound, dyadic zeta convergence and tail bound, finite smooth-number product expansion and squeeze proof, and the finite-product contradiction proving prime-reciprocal divergence. The latter does not substitute $s=1$ into the convergent Euler identity and does not manipulate divergent sums as finite values.

Checked the central-binomial lower bound and the valuation upper bound $p^{v_p(B_n)}\le2n$, yielding the stated explicit prime-counting inequality. Checked the absolutely convergent Möbius-series multiplication, the finite cutoff/uniform-tail proof for the coprime-pair density, finite summation by parts, and the outward-moving-mass counterexample. Optional integral evaluation is labeled as an additional calculus theorem rather than silently used in the earlier proofs. The prime number theorem is clearly stated without a claim of proof. All eight exercise solutions agree with their questions, including factorial valuations of $252$ and the relative-versus-absolute-error counterexample.

Minor terminology suggestion, not a formula error: Section 28.10 says “ordinary convolution of coefficients,” but the displayed regrouping is by products and hence is Dirichlet convolution, not the additive-index Cauchy convolution. The subsequent paragraph correctly identifies Dirichlet convolution. Replacing the phrase with “grouping pairs by their product” would remove any ambiguity.
