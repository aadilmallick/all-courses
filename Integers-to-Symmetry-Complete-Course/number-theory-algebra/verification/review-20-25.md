# Independent mathematical review: Chapters 20 and 25

Reviewed by the author responsible for Chapters 26–30, reading the other authors' chapter sources without editing them. Review date: 2026-11-13. This is a mathematical source review, not a formal proof-assistant certification.

## Chapter 20

Inspected the complete chapter, including linear Diophantine equations, primitive Pythagorean classification, rational parametrization, convergent recurrence and determinant/error identities, Pell existence, fundamental-unit classification, exact continued-fraction states, and practice statements.

No substantive mathematical errors found in the inspected chapter.

The Pell existence proof is complete: pigeonhole approximation supplies infinitely many pairs with bounded nonzero norm; a repeated norm and repeated coordinate residues make a quotient integral; ordering the two positive real representatives produces a norm-one element greater than one and therefore positive integer coordinates. The proof handles negative repeated norms and norm magnitude one correctly. The minimum-positive-unit argument classifies every positive solution using integral inverses and an explicitly justified power reduction.

The continued-fraction periodicity/Pell-index theorem is clearly identified as an additional standard theorem, not inferred from approximation bounds. The independent existence proof does not depend on it. The worked state table for thirteen and the displayed solution 649²−13·180²=1 are arithmetically correct.

Minor wording recommendation, not a mathematical defect: the sentence saying the stated periodicity theorem provides the stronger *fundamental* solution conclusion refers to its standard fuller form; the initial explicit theorem statement itself only asserts a solution at the specified index. It could be made maximally precise by explicitly including “fundamental” in that theorem statement or saying “the stronger standard form of this theorem.”

## Chapter 25

Inspected the complete chapter, including separability/normality, embedding counts, independence of field maps, Artin's fixed-field theorem, finite Galois correspondence, normal-subgroup criterion and quotient identification, biquadratic/S3 examples, finite-field applications and practice statements.

No substantive mathematical errors found in the inspected chapter.

The minimal-support argument in Artin's theorem correctly transforms a nonzero solution with coefficients in the large field into coefficients in the fixed field, proving the difficult upper degree bound. The independent-map argument supplies the reverse inequality after finiteness has been established. The two directions of the correspondence use valid degree comparisons. The normality criterion correctly uses extension of embeddings and the ambient extension's normality. The quotient group is only asserted for normal subgroups.

The examples correctly distinguish E/K being Galois from K/F being Galois. The nonnormal cubic has trivial internal automorphism group and is not described through an invalid S3/C2 quotient. Finite-field fixed degrees and the gcd intersection example are correct.
