# A thousand hours, a language for life

This is an original course for an English-speaking adult beginning French and working toward the broad communicative abilities associated with C1. No previous French or specialist grammar vocabulary is assumed. New grammatical terms are introduced through examples. French examples use standard accents and English translations; translations illustrate meaning, not a claim that every sentence has one unique equivalent.

The ten chapters are substantial teaching chapters. Each supports ten ten-hour learning units. The 1,000 hours include deliberate independent reading and listening, vocabulary review, conversation, writing, revision, projects, and assessment. They are not a claim that this book takes 1,000 hours to read. Take approximately 100 weeks at 10 hours a week, 67 weeks at 15, or 50 weeks at 20, with rest and revision adjusted to your life. Some learners need more time. C1 is not guaranteed by elapsed study time.

The course begins with pronunciation and first conversations, builds the noun phrase and tense system, then develops complex clauses, register, literary recognition, synthesis, and advanced interaction. Its CEFR labels are pedagogical alignments. The CEFR describes language activities and competence; it does not prescribe this particular grammar sequence or a universal French vocabulary list. “Complete” here means a connected course across the requested range, with all ten chapters and working practice tools, not every word, specialist field, regional form, or historical usage in French.

Use the companion for reference-matched grammar, vocabulary, bilingual translation, dictation with meaning, and speaking practice. Every major stage has a shared exercise engine with stage-specific content. Select French text to hear an installed synthetic French voice. Right-click is overridden only when ordinary page text is selected; native menus remain in editable fields. A visible button and Alt+L provide another route. Voices depend on the device; they are not a collection of recorded human speakers.

The speaking workbench includes recording and playback, local speech recognition when a French pack is available, and an optional Chrome on-device AI coach. AI is advisory, can be wrong, and is never used to certify pronunciation or a CEFR level. No API keys, accounts, paid model calls, or course-operated remote AI services are required. The browser may download models, and an online TTS voice may use the operating-system provider’s service. The source notes explain capability limits.

Make a portfolio rather than collecting only scores. Keep early and revised writing, listening diagnoses, short recordings, translations with commentary, and longer projects. Seek periodic feedback from a proficient human speaker. At advanced stages, include real conversations and substantial authentic French media. A complete learning environment must eventually extend beyond a textbook, and the course teaches the habits needed to do that independently.

The canonical chapter files are in `chapters/`; this single volume is assembled from them. The website generates its chapter pages from the same files. The complete archive also contains source, pinned dependency records, exercise data, static build output, a local server, sources, and verification evidence.
