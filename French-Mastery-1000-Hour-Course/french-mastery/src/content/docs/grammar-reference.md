Use this reference when you need a compact explanation of a construction that appears across several chapters. The examples are original. First identify the meaning and grammatical relationship you need; then choose the form. The exercises at the end combine the distinctions rather than testing spelling alone.

## 1. Demonstrative pronouns: choosing a particular one

A demonstrative determiner accompanies a noun: **« ce livre »** — this book. A demonstrative pronoun replaces a noun phrase: **« celui de Nora »** — Nora's one. Choose the pronoun according to the noun it replaces, not the person who owns it.

| Replaced noun | Pronoun | Example and meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Masculine singular | celui | **« Je prends celui de droite. »** — I am taking the one on the right. |
| Feminine singular | celle | **« Ma réponse diffère de celle de Nora. »** — My answer differs from Nora's. |
| Masculine plural | ceux | **« Ceux qui arrivent tôt peuvent entrer. »** — Those who arrive early can enter. |
| Feminine plural | celles | **« Je garde celles que tu préfères. »** — I am keeping the ones you prefer. |

The simple forms normally need a following specification, such as **de + noun**, a relative clause, or another identifying complement. **« Ce stylo fonctionne ; celui de Nora aussi »** identifies a pen through its owner. **« Celui qui fonctionne est sur la table »** identifies it through a relative clause. For a standalone choice, use a compound form: **« Je prends celui-ci »**, rather than a bare **« Je prends celui »**.

Add **-ci** and **-là** to all four forms: *celui-ci, celle-ci, ceux-ci, celles-ci; celui-là, celle-là, ceux-là, celles-là*. They distinguish this one/these ones from that one/those ones. **« Cette veste-ci est légère ; celle-là est plus chaude »** — This jacket is light; that one is warmer. The distinction can be physical, textual, or conversational. In ordinary speech, *-là* is also widely used without a strict contrast in distance. With determiners, the suffix follows the noun: *ce livre-ci*. With pronouns, it attaches directly: *celui-ci*.

Distinguish an identifiable noun from an unspecified idea. **« Celui qui m'intéresse est moins cher »** means the one that interests me is cheaper; a masculine noun such as *modèle* is understood. **« Ce qui m'intéresse, c'est le prix »** means what interests me is the price; *ce* introduces the thing or idea at issue without matching a named noun's gender.

Choose **ce qui** for a relative subject: **« Je comprends ce qui te gêne »** — I understand what bothers you. Choose **ce que** for a direct object: **« Je comprends ce que tu demandes »** — I understand what you are asking for. Choose **ce dont** when the underlying construction needs *de*: **« Je comprends ce dont tu as besoin »** — I understand what you need. Rebuild the basic clause: *quelque chose te gêne; tu demandes quelque chose; tu as besoin de quelque chose*. That reveals the relationship more reliably than translating English *what* mechanically.

## 2. Possessive pronouns: agreement follows the possession

**« Mon dossier »** contains a possessive determiner and a noun. **« Le mien »** replaces the whole phrase. To choose a possessive pronoun, identify the owner, then the gender and number of the thing possessed. If a woman says **« Ce dossier est le mien »**, *mien* is masculine because *dossier* is masculine.

| Owner | One masculine item | One feminine item | Several masculine items | Several feminine items |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| je | le mien | la mienne | les miens | les miennes |
| tu | le tien | la tienne | les tiens | les tiennes |
| il/elle | le sien | la sienne | les siens | les siennes |
| nous | le nôtre | la nôtre | les nôtres | les nôtres |
| vous | le vôtre | la vôtre | les vôtres | les vôtres |
| ils/elles | le leur | la leur | les leurs | les leurs |

**« Vos conclusions sont proches des miennes »** — Your conclusions are close to mine. *Conclusions* is feminine plural, so *les miennes* is required; *de + les* contracts to *des*. **« Je préfère votre méthode à la leur »** — I prefer your method to theirs. **« Son bureau est plus grand que le nôtre »** — His or her office is larger than ours. *Son* alone does not establish whether the owner is male or female.

Keep the circumflex in the pronouns **nôtre** and **vôtre**. The determiners have no circumflex: **« notre projet »**, **« votre réponse »**. Compare **« Notre projet est prêt ; le vôtre avance »** — Our project is ready; yours is progressing. Do not write *le votre* in edited standard French. Pronouns normally keep their article; **« C'est mien »** is not the ordinary equivalent of *It is mine*. Use **« C'est le mien »** for an identified masculine object or **« C'est à moi »** to express ownership without choosing a gendered replacement.

## 3. Adverbs: forming them and choosing their scope

An adjective describes a noun; an adverb commonly modifies a verb, adjective, another adverb, or a whole statement. **« Une réponse claire »** is a clear answer; **« Elle répond clairement »** means she answers clearly. Many manner adverbs use **-ment**, often added to the feminine adjective: *clair → claire → clairement; heureux → heureuse → heureusement; doux → douce → doucement*. An adjective already ending in *e* commonly adds *-ment* directly: *rare → rarement*.

For many masculine adjectives ending in **-é, -i, or -u**, add *-ment* to that masculine form: *aisé → aisément; poli → poliment; absolu → absolument*. Frequent **-ant** adjectives give **-amment**: *constant → constamment; suffisant → suffisamment*. Frequent **-ent** adjectives give **-emment**: *prudent → prudemment; apparent → apparemment*. In these endings, *-amment* and *-emment* are generally pronounced alike, so sound alone does not settle their spelling. These formation patterns are summarized in the [CCDMD adverb reference](https://francomane.ccdmd.qc.ca/sites/man.ccdmd.qc.ca/files/documents/synthese_les_adverbes_0_1.pdf) and the [OQLF formation guide](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/21700/lorthographe/cas-particuliers-lies-a-lorthographe/formation-des-adverbes-en-ment).

Do not turn patterns into an exception-free machine. Learn *lent → lentement*, *gentil → gentiment*, *bref → brièvement*, *précis → précisément*, and *profond → profondément*. *Bon* commonly corresponds to *bien*: **« Elle explique bien »** — She explains well. *Mauvais* commonly corresponds to *mal*: **« Il a mal compris »** — He misunderstood. *Vite* is an adverb in **« Elle marche vite »**; it does not become feminine or plural to match *elle*.

Position depends on length, function, and focus. A short frequent adverb often follows a simple finite verb: **« Je comprends bien »**. In a compound tense it often falls between auxiliary and participle: **« J'ai bien compris »**, **« Elle a souvent voyagé »**. A longer manner expression can follow the participle: **« Elle a expliqué la procédure très clairement »**. Sentence adverbs can frame the statement: **« Heureusement, personne n'a été blessé »** — Fortunately, nobody was injured.

Placement can change meaning. **« Il a simplement expliqué le problème »** may mean he merely explained it; **« Il a expliqué le problème simplement »** means he explained it in a simple way. **« Elle travaille encore »** means she is still working; **« Elle travaille seulement le lundi »** restricts the working day. Ask what the adverb modifies before deciding its location. Adverbs do not all share one compulsory slot; see the [OQLF overview of adverb functions](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/24328/la-grammaire/la-grammaire-actuelle/les-classes-de-mots-et-les-groupes/ladverbe-et-le-groupe-adverbial) for a continuing reference.

## 4. Indefinites and free choice

**Chaque** accompanies a singular noun; **chacun/chacune** replaces a person or item considered individually. **« Chaque candidate reçoit un dossier »** — Each candidate receives a file. **« Chacune reçoit un dossier »** — Each one receives a file. Use a singular verb in these examples. **« Chacun des participants a répondu »** distributes the action across the group without turning *chacun* into a plural subject. **« Tout le monde a répondu »** also takes singular agreement, despite referring to several people.

**Quelqu'un** means someone; **quelque chose** means something. An adjective after these constructions often uses **de**: **« quelqu'un de fiable »** — someone reliable; **« quelque chose d'utile »** — something useful. With *quelque chose*, the adjective is masculine singular in this construction: **« quelque chose de nouveau »**. Similarly, **« rien d'important »** means nothing important. Do not infer feminine agreement from the feminine noun *chose* inside the fixed indefinite expression.

In standard negative statements, use **personne/rien/aucun** with *ne*, normally without adding *pas*: **« Personne ne vient »**, **« Je n'ai rien reçu »**, **« Aucun dossier n'est complet »**. *Aucun* accompanies the singular noun here; *aucun des dossiers* can replace it. **« Personne n'a rien dit »** means nobody said anything; the negatives combine naturally. Learn the whole construction, rather than treating each word as an isolated English equivalent: **« sans rien dire »** means without saying anything. **« Est-ce que quelqu'un a vu quoi que ce soit ? »** asks whether anyone saw anything, without asserting that nobody did.

**Tout** can mean everything: **« Tout est prêt »**. **Tous/toutes** can mean all the members of an established group: **« Ils sont tous arrivés »**, **« Elles sont toutes arrivées »**. Before nouns, agreement follows the noun: *tout le dossier, toute la page, tous les dossiers, toutes les pages*. Distinguish **« tous les jours »** — every day — from **« toute la journée »** — all day.

Free-choice expressions widen the selection. **« N'importe qui peut participer »** — Anyone can take part. **« Choisis n'importe quoi »** — Choose anything. In **« Tu dis n'importe quoi »**, the expression can instead criticize the content as nonsense; context matters. **N'importe lequel/laquelle/lesquels/lesquelles** selects freely from a known set: **« Tu peux prendre n'importe laquelle de ces chaises »** — You can take any of these chairs. **« N'importe quel siège convient »** uses a determiner plus noun. **« Quel que soit votre choix »** — Whatever your choice — is a separate construction with subjunctive *soit*; its form should not be confused with *quelque chose*.

## 5. Future anterior and recent past: locating one event relative to another

The **futur antérieur** consists of the future of *avoir* or *être* plus a past participle: **« j'aurai terminé »**, **« tu auras compris »**, **« elle sera partie »**, **« nous serons arrivés »**. Choose the auxiliary as for the corresponding compound past construction and apply its agreement rules. **« Les lettres que j'aurai écrites »** has agreement with preceding direct object *lettres*; **« elles seront arrivées »** has agreement with the subject through *être*.

Think of a future reference point and look backward from it. **« Demain à dix heures, j'aurai terminé »** — By ten tomorrow, I will have finished. The finishing occurs before the future checkpoint. **« Quand tu arriveras, nous aurons déjà mangé »** — When you arrive, we will already have eaten. Arrival is future; eating is completed before that arrival. The future anterior does not require a second verb if an expression such as *demain à dix heures* supplies the checkpoint.

French generally retains a future tense after future-time conjunctions such as **quand, lorsque, dès que** where English commonly uses present: **« Dès que j'arriverai, je t'appellerai »** — As soon as I arrive, I will call you. Use the future anterior when completion matters: **« Dès que j'aurai terminé le rapport, je te l'enverrai »** — As soon as I have finished the report, I will send it to you. Do not import English tense choices word for word. This rule concerns future time clauses; conditional *si* follows a different system.

The **passé récent** uses **venir de + infinitive**. **« Je viens de terminer »** — I have just finished. Conjugate *venir* for the person: *je viens, tu viens, il vient, nous venons, vous venez, ils viennent*. The infinitive remains unchanged. **« Elles viennent de partir »** means they have just left; it is not *elles viennent de parties*. Distinguish physical origin: **« Je viens de Lyon »** — I come from Lyon — has a place, not an infinitive.

From a past viewpoint, use the imperfect of *venir*: **« Je venais de terminer quand elle a appelé »** — I had just finished when she called. First the work finishes; then, soon afterward, the call occurs. *Venais de* marks recency relative to that past moment, not necessarily to now. **« Ils venaient de s'installer »** — They had just moved in — puts the reflexive pronoun before the infinitive. These constructions help narrate immediate transitions without pretending that *just* always translates to one isolated French adverb.

## 6. Prepositions in space, transport, and time

For many ordinary cities, use **à** for location or destination and **de** for origin: **« Je vais à Paris ; je reviens de Paris »**. For countries, learn the article and then the common pattern:

| Country type | Location/destination | Origin |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Feminine | en France | de France |
| Masculine beginning with a consonant | au Canada | du Canada |
| Masculine beginning with a vowel | en Iran | d'Iran |
| Plural | aux États-Unis | des États-Unis |

**« Elle habite en France et travaille au Canada »** states locations; movement does not require a different preposition in these examples. Some names have conventional patterns outside the basic table: *à Cuba, de Cuba; à Madagascar, de Madagascar*. Learn uncertain place names as complete expressions rather than guessing from an ending. City names containing an article can also require contraction, as in *au Havre*.

For transport, the course's neutral patterns are **en voiture, en train, en bus, en avion, en bateau**, but **à pied, à vélo, à moto, à cheval**. **« Je vais au bureau à vélo »** describes the means of travel. **« Je suis dans le bus »** describes being inside the bus; **« Le sac est sur le vélo »** locates the bag on the bicycle. A physical-location phrase and a transport expression answer different questions.

Time expressions encode different relationships. **Depuis** connects an earlier start to a reference point: **« J'habite ici depuis deux ans »** — I have lived here for two years; **« depuis lundi »** gives the starting date. French uses present because the residence continues now. With a past reference, **« Il attendait depuis une heure quand je suis arrivé »** means he had been waiting for an hour when I arrived.

**Pendant** measures a bounded period: **« J'ai habité ici pendant deux ans »** — I lived here for two years. It does not by itself assert continuation now. **Il y a** places something before now: **« Je suis arrivé il y a deux ans »** — I arrived two years ago. **Dans** places an event after now: **« Je partirai dans deux heures »** — I will leave in two hours. Compare **« Je partirai pour deux semaines »** — I will go away for two weeks — which gives the planned duration, not the delay before leaving. Before translating English *for*, draw the start, duration, and endpoint you actually mean.

## 7. Tout before an adjective: intensity or all members?

When **tout** means *completely* or *very*, it functions adverbially. It normally stays unchanged before a masculine adjective, a feminine adjective beginning with a vowel, or a feminine adjective beginning with a **mute h**. The exception is a feminine adjective beginning with a consonant or **aspirated h**: *tout* then agrees in gender and number. The *h* distinction affects liaison and elision; an aspirated *h* is not normally an instruction to pronounce an English-style h sound.

Original contrasts: **« Ils sont tout surpris »** — They are completely surprised; **« Elle est tout étonnée »** — She is completely astonished; **« Elles sont tout heureuses »** — They are very happy; **« Elle est toute contente »** — She is very pleased; **« Elles sont toutes honteuses »** — They are thoroughly ashamed. *Heureux* has mute *h*; *honteux* has aspirated *h*. Check a dictionary when uncertain.

Compare the pronoun meaning *all*: **« Ils sont tous heureux »** means all of them are happy, whereas **« Ils sont tout heureux »** means they are very happy. In **« Elles sont toutes contentes »**, spelling alone can permit both all-members and intensity readings. If the distinction matters, write **« Elles sont toutes très contentes »** or **« Elles sont extrêmement contentes »**. The standard agreement rule is confirmed by [OQLF's adverbial tout reference](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/22907/la-grammaire/ladverbe/tout-employe-comme-adverbe) and the [Government of Canada writing guide](https://nos-langues.canada.ca/fr/cles-de-la-redaction/tout-accord-de-ladverbe).

## 8. Twelve exercises, with an explained key

1. Replace *la réponse de Nora*: **« Ma réponse diffère de ___. »**
2. Translate: *I am taking this one, not that one*, referring to two masculine books.
3. Complete: **« Je comprends ce ___ tu demandes et ce ___ tu as besoin. »**
4. Replace *nos conclusions*: **« Vos conclusions ressemblent à ___. »** Preserve any contraction.
5. Form the adverbs from *prudent, constant, gentil,* and *lent*.
6. Translate: *She has often traveled, and she explained the procedure clearly.*
7. Complete: **« ___ des candidates reçoit quelque chose ___ utile. »** Use *each one*.
8. Translate: *Nobody received anything. You can take any of these chairs.*
9. Complete: **« Dès que j'___ terminé, je t'___ le fichier. »** Use the future anterior of *terminer* and the simple future of *envoyer*.
10. Translate: *I had just left when she called.*
11. Complete: **« Je vais ___ Canada ___ avion. J'y habite ___ deux ans ; je suis arrivé ___ deux ans. »**
12. Complete with intensity meanings: **« Elles sont ___ heureuses, ___ contentes et ___ étonnées. »** Then translate *All of them are happy* with a masculine group.

**1.** **« celle de Nora »**. *Réponse* is feminine singular; *celle* takes its identifying complement. **2.** **« Je prends celui-ci, pas celui-là. »** The suffixes make the choices independently identifiable. **3.** **« ce que tu demandes et ce dont tu as besoin »**. *Demander* takes a direct object here; *avoir besoin de* supplies the relationship represented by *dont*.

**4.** **« aux nôtres »**. Feminine plural *conclusions* gives *les nôtres*; *à + les* contracts to *aux*. Keep the circumflex. **5.** *Prudemment, constamment, gentiment, lentement.* The first two follow productive endings; the latter two must be learned with their actual forms. **6.** **« Elle a souvent voyagé et elle a expliqué la procédure clairement. »** *Souvent* naturally sits inside the compound tense; the manner adverb can follow the object. Other well-formed placements may shift focus.

**7.** **« Chacune des candidates reçoit quelque chose d'utile. »** *Chacune* is feminine singular; *de* elides before *utile*. **8.** **« Personne n'a rien reçu. Tu peux prendre n'importe laquelle de ces chaises. »** The negative words combine without *pas*. *Laquelle* matches feminine singular *chaise*, since you select one chair from the set.

**9.** **« Dès que j'aurai terminé, je t'enverrai le fichier. »** Completion precedes the future sending. **10.** **« Je venais de partir quand elle a appelé. »** The recent past is located relative to a past call. **11.** **« au Canada en avion... depuis deux ans... il y a deux ans »**. Destination, transport, ongoing duration, and past starting event each need their own expression.

**12.** **« tout heureuses, toutes contentes et tout étonnées »**. Mute *h* and a vowel preserve the adverb's invariable form; feminine consonant-initial *contentes* requires agreement. **« Ils sont tous heureux »** expresses all members of the masculine group. The explanation must identify both grammatical function and following sound; copying the adjective's ending everywhere would give the wrong result.

## Reference and verification note

### A final set of distinctions for careful reading

**Neutral le** can stand for an idea, a property, or a clause rather than a masculine noun. In **« Elle est prête, et je le suis aussi »**, “She is ready, and I am too,” **le** represents being ready; it stays **le** even when the speaker is a woman. Compare **« Je la connais »**, “I know her,” where **la** represents a feminine person or noun. In **« Il viendra, je le sais »**, “He will come; I know it,” **le** represents the proposition. Identify what a pronoun replaces before assigning it a gender mechanically.

**Soi** is a stressed reflexive pronoun useful with a general or indefinite subject: **« On a parfois besoin de temps pour soi »**, “Sometimes one needs time for oneself.” With a particular person, use the appropriate stressed form: **« Elle pense à elle-même »**, “She is thinking about herself.” **« Chacun pour soi »**, “everyone for themselves,” is a fixed useful example. Conversational **on** meaning a specific “we” can behave differently from a fully general **on**, so inspect the intended reference rather than substituting **soi** for every pronoun after on.

For **color adjectives**, ordinary simple adjectives generally agree: **« des robes bleues »**, “blue dresses.” Many noun-derived color terms remain invariable: **« des chaussures marron »**, “brown shoes,” and **« des robes orange »**, “orange dresses.” Some established exceptions, including **rose**, agree: **« des fleurs roses »**, “pink flowers.” A compound color description normally remains invariable: **« des yeux bleu clair »**, “light-blue eyes.” Keep the distinction between a color adjective and a noun used as a color label; a rule such as “all colors are invariable” loses it.

**Pire** is an adjective meaning “worse,” contrasting with **meilleur**, “better.” **« Cette solution est pire que la première »** means “This solution is worse than the first.” **Plus mauvais** is also possible in many literal comparisons. **Moindre** can mean smaller or lesser, often with abstract nouns: **« un risque moindre »**, “a smaller risk.” For an ordinary physical size comparison, **plus petit** is usually the straightforward choice. Do not confuse adjective **pire** with adverb **pis** in fixed expressions such as **tant pis**, “too bad.”

**Quoique** means “although” and commonly introduces a subjunctive clause: **« Quoique ce soit difficile, nous continuons »**, “Although it is difficult, we continue.” **Quoi que** means “whatever,” as in **« Quoi que tu décides, préviens-moi »**, “Whatever you decide, let me know.” Their similar sound does not make their functions identical. **Quel que**, with forms agreeing with its subject, appears in **« Quelle que soit la raison, il faut répondre »**, “Whatever the reason, we must reply.” Contrast **quelque** as a determiner in **« quelque difficulté »**, “some difficulty,” or plural **quelques difficultés**, “a few difficulties.” Ask first whether the sentence means “although,” “whatever thing,” “whatever the identified noun may be,” or “some/a few.” That meaning test is more reliable than guessing the spelling from sound.
