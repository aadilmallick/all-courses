# Business templates for the first small-tool funnel

These are **proposed learner templates**, not live offers or messages. Descriptive fields are intentional: replace them with real, verified details before publishing or sending. Do not invent a sender address, postal address, testimonial, customer result, delivery date, or working download link. The first useful offer should fit the same 8–10 hours per week as the videos.

## 2. Free-resource page copy

**Finish your small browser tool with fewer avoidable mistakes**

Get the two-page checklist from the channel's small-tool walkthroughs. It helps you check persistence, empty states, recovery, and the path from one working demo to a usable tool.

For AI-powered versions, the output-review box asks whether the structure is valid, important claims can be traced to the input, and missing evidence produces a useful response.

The resource includes one completed example and a clear test you can run on your own project. It is intended for developers who can already build a small JavaScript interface. It is a project aid, not a promise of store approval or production security.

**Email field**

**Button: Send me the checklist**

By signing up, you request the checklist and a short welcome series, followed by a practical engineering email about once a week. Some messages introduce my paid notes or project help. You can unsubscribe at any time. See the privacy notice for how your address is handled.

Before use: connect the actual resource, accurate privacy notice, sender identity, and functioning subscription controls. Test the complete experience on a phone and with a separate browser profile. See Chapter 8 for seven complete example welcome messages and relevant official guidance.

## 3. Notebook product brief and page structure

**Proposed product:** *Ship a Small Developer Tool — Annotated Notebook*.

**Proposed buyer task:** Understand and reuse the reasoning behind a small browser-tool implementation, rather than repeatedly searching through a long video.

**Example introductory copy:** “A practical reference for the small-tool walkthroughs: one complete example, annotated engineering decisions, persistence and error-state tests, and a troubleshooting map. Preview a full sample section before deciding whether it fits your current level.”

The finished page should show the actual files, tested environment, prerequisite knowledge, a readable sample, price, version/date, update promise, refund terms, and support route. The proposed $49 used in the course is a scenario price, not a verified market rate. Do not promise lifetime updates to every changing AI tool.

**Sample review protocol:** Ask a suitable test reader to find the explanation of a failed reload, identify the function involved, and describe the next diagnostic step. Record where the reader gets stuck. Revise the sample rather than relying only on “looks good” feedback.

## 4. Bounded tutoring or project-review offer

**Proposed headline:** One focused session to understand the next obstacle in your small tool.

**Example offer copy:** “Bring one agreed problem in a small web or browser project. We will clarify the expected behavior, inspect a minimal example, test a hypothesis, and identify the next steps. The proposed session is sixty minutes live, with up to fifteen minutes of preparation and a concise follow-up note. It is suitable when you want to understand the reasoning and continue the work yourself.”

**Scope boundary:** The session does not include an entire product implementation, unlimited asynchronous debugging, work outside the confirmed topic, or completion of prohibited assessment work. Additional work needs a separate agreement. A session should be confirmed only when the problem fits the provider's competence and available time.

**Intake questions:**

1. What are you trying to make happen?
2. What happens instead, and how can you reproduce it?
3. What have you already tried?
4. Which part of the reasoning is unclear?
5. What would make one session useful?
6. Is the material yours to share, and can you provide a minimal example without secrets?

**Delivery note:** State the observed problem, the tested hypothesis, what changed, the remaining limitation, and the learner's next action. A useful note preserves the reasoning rather than promising that every related problem is solved.

The course's $150/session is an illustrative price. Confirm the actual scope, scheduling, cancellation, payment, and support arrangements before booking. Include total preparation and follow-up in the calendar.

## 5. Fixed-scope engineering audit outline

**Proposed buyer decision:** Decide which reliability or usability issue in one small browser tool deserves attention first.

**Proposed deliverables:** A prioritized written review, a walkthrough of the evidence, and a short list of next actions. A code patch is included only if explicitly agreed. The scope should identify the supported project, supplied materials, test conditions, review depth, delivery date, revision boundary, and acceptance process.

**Example scope paragraph:** “Review the agreed onboarding and data-flow path for one small browser tool using the supplied demo and architecture description. Deliver a concise report identifying up to three consequential issues, the evidence observed, and proposed next steps. Discuss the report in one scheduled walkthrough. This scope does not include a comprehensive penetration test, legal certification, ongoing maintenance, or unlimited implementation work.”

This is a planning outline, not a ready legal agreement. Use an appropriate agreement for actual client work and obtain relevant professional advice where the stakes require it. The course's $1,500 and six-hour total-work assumptions are illustrative. Establish competence, fit, and scope before quoting.

## 6. Community demand test

Before creating a recurring subscription, test one recurring activity. A proposed four-week project clinic might include one weekly group session, one defined feedback thread, and a specific response window. State the audience level and the kind of project it serves.

Record attendance, returning participants, recurring questions, useful progress, moderator time, and requests for continued activity. Ask why someone returned or stopped attending. A successful free event does not automatically validate a paid recurring service; a paid pilot with clear terms provides different evidence.

**Proposed recurring promise:** “Finish and explain one small tool with structured feedback and peers working at a similar level.” Avoid selling unlimited access to the creator. Do not add a paid community to an already full production schedule without measuring the obligation.

## 7. Offer evidence log

| Date/window | Observation | Source/permission | Interpretation | Alternative explanation | Next test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Learner fills a real date | Exact observed behavior, anonymized if needed | Public source or appropriate permission | Clearly marked inference | Another plausible cause | One bounded action |

Examples of useful observations include a reader finding the correct notebook section, a buyer asking a recurring setup question, or an inquiry describing a specific decision an audit could support. Do not create synthetic testimonials from these examples. Obtain separate permission before quoting real feedback publicly.

## 8. Monthly economics record

Keep observed values separate from scenarios. For each stream record quantity, price, gross sales excluding tax collected for others, refunds, actual fees, cash costs, payout timing, and owner hours. Record unique people separately from orders and sessions.

The shared scenario is $1,000 ads + $2,940 notes + $1,560 community + $1,500 tutoring + $3,000 audits = $10,000 gross. Its modeled $8,445 pre-tax operating surplus follows explicit hypothetical allowances in Chapter 9. It is not take-home income or a ninety-day expectation.

**Monthly decision note:** “The strongest observed demand was ___. The largest unsupported assumption remains ___. Delivery used ___ hours. The main source of support confusion was ___. Next month I will test ___ while keeping ___ stable.” These blanks are deliberate template fields. Use real observations rather than filling them with the course's hypothetical conversion rates.
