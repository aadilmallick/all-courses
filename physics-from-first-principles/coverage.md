# Coverage and source crosswalk

This course is organized for a beginner rather than in Feynman's lecture order. A mapping identifies related treatment; it does **not** mean every specialized application or argument in a source lecture has been reproduced. “Worked core” refers to original explanations and derivations of the central subject, not a promise of exhaustive source equivalence. “Selected” and “contextual” rows explicitly identify narrower coverage.

The supplied PDF outline contains 114 numbered teaching chapters across Volumes I–III. The current official online contents have the additional II.42 chapter. PDF page references below are one-based physical PDF pages, not printed chapter-page labels. The source audit was partial, as detailed in [sources.md](sources.md).

## Coverage against a physics education

| Subject | Original course treatment | Practice and evidence route |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Beginner mathematical prerequisites | 01–03, then new tools locally throughout | Foundational worked examples and problems |
| Introductory and analytical mechanics | 04–07, 10–11 | Conservation, rotation, oscillators, action and Hamiltonian examples |
| Waves and optics | 08–09 | Fourier, mode, interference, diffraction and polarization problems |
| Continuum, thermal and statistical physics | 12–14 | Fluids, elasticity, diffusion, engines, ensembles and probabilities |
| Electromagnetism | 15–18 | Fields, boundary problems, circuits, induction, wave energy |
| Undergraduate quantum foundations | 19–22 | Amplitudes, wave mechanics, angular momentum, approximation and scattering |
| Modern subject introductions | 23–25 | Bands, atomic/nuclear/particle foundations and gravitation/cosmology |
| Graduate preparation | 26 | Selected worked tools; further graduate coursework remains necessary |
| Computation and experimental inference | 27–28 | Numerical checks, regression, uncertainty, labs and capstones |

The 23 supplied browser experiments implement selected demonstrable models across every chapter family. They are not substitutes for physical apparatus or all assigned projects. In particular, RLC fitting, double-well coding, actual spectral analysis and physical laboratory measurements are learner projects, not precompleted companion features. Specialist courses in advanced electrodynamics, many-body theory, quantum field theory, plasma physics, astrophysics and general relativity require further sustained study.

## Source-dependent limits

Color perception, atmospheric electricity, engineering waveguides, particular maser and hyperfine devices, magnetic-material catalogs and historical electromagnetic-mass discussions are contextual or selected references rather than full duplicate treatments. The course develops the mathematical and physical foundations needed to approach them. Original source reading remains valuable for those specialized examples.

No topic list can certify a reader's competence. The [study milestones](curriculum.md), independent problem work and capstones supply ways to assess progress. The [verification report](verification.md) reports checks on this artifact, not an outcome study of learners.
