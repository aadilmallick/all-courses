# Chapter 28 worked solutions

1. Mean is 2.00 s. Deviations are $-0.02,0,+0.02$ s; squared deviations sum to 0.0008 s². Sample variance is 0.0004 s² and standard deviation 0.020 s. The estimated standard error is $0.020/\sqrt3\approx0.01155$ s. This inference assumes independent comparable measurements of a stable quantity; shared calibration uncertainty requires an additional term.
2. Symmetry gives mean zero. Variance is $q^{-1}\int_{-q/2}^{q/2}x^2dx=q^{-1}[x^3/3]_{-q/2}^{q/2}=q^2/12$. Taking its square root gives $q/\sqrt{12}$. The result depends on a uniform distribution within the bin.
3. The sensitivities are $\partial g/\partial L=g/L$ and $\partial g/\partial T=-2g/T$. For independent inputs, variance is $g^2[(\sigma_L/L)^2+4(\sigma_T/T)^2]$. The factor two arises from the power $T^{-2}$, so the variance contribution contains its square. Correlated inputs would add the covariance term.
4. For additive offset, $(x_2+b)-(x_1+b)=x_2-x_1$. For scale $1+\epsilon$, $(1+\epsilon)x_2-(1+\epsilon)x_1=(1+\epsilon)(x_2-x_1)$. The scale remains. This is why a common zero error and a common calibration factor have different effects on differences.
5. The means are 0.475 m and 1.94 s². Centred squared-length sum is 0.1125 m²; centred product sum is 0.4500 m s². The slope is 4.00 s²/m and intercept 0.040 s². Residuals are $(-0.01,0.01,0.01,-0.01)$ s², giving $S=0.0004$ s⁴. With two residual degrees of freedom, $s^2=0.0002$ s⁴ and $s_b=\sqrt{0.0002/0.1125}\approx0.04216$ s²/m. Under the illustrative model, $g=\pi^2\approx9.86960$ m/s² and its first-order standard error is about 0.1040 m/s².
6. Linearizing $Y=T^2$ gives $\delta Y\approx2T\delta T$, hence $\sigma_Y\approx2T\sigma_T$. Longer periods therefore have larger absolute squared-period uncertainty if time uncertainty is fixed. Weights proportional to $1/(4T^2\sigma_T^2)$ reflect that approximate variance model. Large relative errors require more than linear propagation.
7. Four points minus three fitted independent parameters leave only one nominal residual degree of freedom. Many flexible curves can match such a small sample, and fit residuals alone test little. Collect new points outside the fitted sample, vary a condition the model predicts independently, calibrate parameters separately where possible, inspect residual structure, and compare a plausible alternative model. Avoid claiming validation from fitted agreement alone.
8. One possible plan asks whether pendulum period changes with amplitude by an amount resolvable by the apparatus. Record a fixed length, several initial angles and repeated multi-cycle times. Compare the nonlinear and small-angle predictions using a converged numerical model. Budget angle, length, timing, drift and bob-geometry uncertainty. Deliver raw observations, model derivation, convergence results, fitted or directly compared periods, residuals and a stated effect bound. An inconclusive result is legitimate if the predicted period change is smaller than the combined uncertainty or confounded with drift; report the bound rather than inventing a detection.
