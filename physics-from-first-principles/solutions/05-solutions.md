# 05. Complete solutions

1. $W=\int_0^3 4x\,dx=2x^2|_0^3=18\,\mathrm J$. The final kinetic energy equals this work, so $v=\sqrt{2W/m}=\sqrt{18}=4.24\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$.
2. Initial energy is $\tfrac12(100)(0.080)^2=0.320\,\mathrm J$. The resistance does $-(0.50)(0.40)=-0.200\,\mathrm J$ work. Final kinetic energy is $0.120\,\mathrm J$, so $v=\sqrt{2(0.120)/0.20}=\sqrt{1.2}=1.095\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. The stipulated full release avoids retaining a spring-energy term at the endpoint.
3. Initial momentum is $4.0\,\mathrm{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$. Sticking gives $V=4/4=1.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. Initial kinetic energy is $8.0\,\mathrm J$ and final is $2.0\,\mathrm J$, a change of $-6.0\,\mathrm J$. For an elastic collision, $v_1=[(1-3)/(1+3)]4=-2.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ and $v_2=[2(1)/(1+3)]4=2.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. Final momentum is $-2+6=4$ and energy is $2+6=8$ in SI.
4. With negligible external impulse, the center-of-mass velocity $\mathbf V$ is unchanged. Initially $K_i=\tfrac12MV^2+\tfrac12\mu|\mathbf u_1-\mathbf u_2|^2$. After sticking, both objects move at $\mathbf V$, so $K_f=\tfrac12MV^2$. Subtraction gives the stated loss. This argument assumes constant masses and Newtonian kinetic energy; the energy becomes internal and other accounted forms rather than disappearing.
5. $\Delta v=3\ln4=4.159\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$. Doubling the ratio to 8 gives $3\ln8=6.238\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$, not $8.318$. The logarithm obeys $\ln(2R)=\ln2+\ln R$, whereas doubling the velocity increment would require squaring the ratio: $\ln R^2=2\ln R$.
6. $GM/r=4.9825\times10^7\,\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-2}}$. Thus $v_{\rm circ}\approx7.059\times10^3\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ and $v_{\rm esc}\approx9.983\times10^3\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. $T=2\pi(8.00\times10^6)/7058.68\approx7121\,\mathrm s$, about $118.7$ minutes.
7. $U'=4\alpha x(x^2-a^2)$ gives equilibria $x=0,\pm a$. $U''=4\alpha(3x^2-a^2)$ is negative at zero and positive at $\pm a$. Hence zero is unstable and $\pm a$ are stable minima. Their potential is zero; the central maximum has $U(0)=\alpha a^4$, the barrier height. For total energy below this barrier, the two accessible wells are disconnected in classical motion.
8. At the top of an inside track, inward points downward. For an ordinary contact that pushes inward at the top, $N+mg=mv^2/R$. Contact requires $N\geq0$, so $v^2\geq gR$. Starting at rest at the top would require $N=-mg$, impossible for that unattached inside contact. The particle immediately loses contact rather than following the circular path. Energy alone can produce a speed along an assumed path, but it does not enforce the sign of the support force needed to maintain that path.
