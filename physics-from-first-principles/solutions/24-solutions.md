# 24. Worked solutions

1. $dE/dZ_*=2Z_*-2Z+5/8=0$, giving $Z_*=Z-5/16$ when this is positive. The minimum is $-(Z-5/16)^2$ Hartree. The trial state restricts both spatial orbitals to the same exponential and omits explicit interelectronic-distance correlation, so it is a variational approximation to the specified Hamiltonian.
2. Intensity is proportional to $[(1/2\tau)^2+(\omega-\omega_0)^2]^{-1}$. At the stated detuning the denominator doubles, so intensity is half its peak. The half-width at half maximum is $1/(2\tau)$; the distance between the two half-maximum points is $1/\tau$.
3. $B=0.0500\times931.5\approx46.6\,\mathrm{MeV}$, and $B/A\approx6.65\,\mathrm{MeV}$ per nucleon.
4. The fraction is $2^{-2.5}\approx0.1768$. With half-life $7200\,\mathrm s$, $\lambda=\ln2/7200\approx9.63\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$.
5. $Q=1.50-2(0.511)=0.478\,\mathrm{MeV}$. The positive result makes the channel kinematically possible, subject to nuclear-state and interaction constraints.
6. The cross section is $2.00\times10^{-30}\,\mathrm{m}^2$. The interaction probability is $10^{-6}$, giving $20\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ interactions and $5.0\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ detected events on average. The thin-target condition is satisfied.
7. $K_{\mathrm{th}}=[4^2-(1+2)^2]/(2\times2)=7/4=1.75$ in the chosen energy units. The rest-energy deficit is $4-3=1$; the additional $0.75$ supplies unavoidable final center-of-mass motion in the laboratory.
8. $\sin^2(2\theta)=\sin^2(\pi/3)=3/4$ and the phase factor is one, so probability is $0.75$. Changing baseline changes relative phase and therefore produces oscillatory transition probability, as long as coherent propagation and the other approximations remain valid.
