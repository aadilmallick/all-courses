# 26. Worked solutions

1. Acting on a test function, $\int\delta(bx)f(x)dx=(1/|b|)\int\delta(u)f(u/b)du=f(0)/|b|$. This equals the action of $\delta(x)/|b|$. Since the integral must return the units of $f$, the delta's units cancel those of $dx$.
2. With $G=Ax$ left and $G=B(L-x)$ right, continuity gives $A\xi=B(L-\xi)$ and the source integral gives $A+B=1$. Hence $A=(L-\xi)/L$, $B=\xi/L$, producing $G=\min(x,\xi)[L-\max(x,\xi)]/L$. The function vanishes at both boundaries and its slope jump is $-1$.
3. $x(t)=\Theta(t)\sin(\omega_0t)/\omega_0$. It is zero before the impulse, continuous at zero, and has $\dot x(0^+)-\dot x(0^-)=1$. If the original equation were $m\ddot x+m\omega_0^2x=J\delta(t)$, the velocity jump would instead be $J/m$.
4. $\delta S=\int[-\partial^\mu\phi\partial_\mu\eta-\kappa^2\phi\eta]d^4x=\int(\partial_\mu\partial^\mu\phi-\kappa^2\phi)\eta d^4x-\int_{\partial\Omega}n_\mu\partial^\mu\phi\eta d\Sigma$. The boundary term vanishes for variations zero on the boundary. Arbitrary interior $\eta$ then gives the field equation.
5. Initially $\rho=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}$. At $p=1/4$, $\rho'=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&1/4\\1/4&1/2\end{pmatrix}$, with purity $2(1/4)+2(1/16)=5/8$. At $p=1/2$, the state is $I/2$ with purity $1/2$. At $p=1$, it is the pure $|-x\rangle$ state, with off-diagonal entries $-1/2$ and purity one.
6. $Z=e^{\beta\Delta/2}+e^{-\beta\Delta/2}=2\cosh(\beta\Delta/2)$. Its first logarithmic derivative gives $U=-(\Delta/2)\tanh(\beta\Delta/2)$, and temperature differentiation gives $C=k_B(\beta\Delta/2)^2\operatorname{sech}^2(\beta\Delta/2)$. The lower-state probability is $1/(1+e^{-\beta\Delta})$, or about $0.8808$ at $\beta\Delta=2$.
7. $F''=a(T-T_c)+3bm^2$. At either nonzero point, substituting $bm^2=a(T_c-T)$ gives $F''=2a(T_c-T)>0$, so both are local minima. The zero point has negative curvature below $T_c$.
8. Equal units of $r_0\phi^2$ and $K|\nabla\phi|^2$ imply $K/r_0$ has units length squared. As $r_0\to0^+$, the Gaussian correlation length diverges. If $r_0<0$ without a stabilizing higher-order term, a uniform field can lower this truncated free energy without bound, so the assumed stable Gaussian phase no longer exists.
