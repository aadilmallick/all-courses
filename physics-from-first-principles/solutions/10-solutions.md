# 10. Complete solutions

1. $\gamma=1/\sqrt{1-0.80^2}=5/3$. Laboratory time is $(5/3)(3.0)=5.0\,\mu\mathrm s$. Distance is $(0.80)(3.00\times10^8)(5.0\times10^{-6})=1200\,\mathrm m$ using rounded $c$.
2. At $0.60c$, $\gamma=1.25$. Thus $L=L_0/\gamma=12/1.25=9.6\,\mathrm m$. This uses the positions of the two endpoints recorded at one laboratory time, not two events simultaneous in the rod frame and not automatically a photograph.
3. $c\Delta t=600\,\mathrm m<900\,\mathrm m$, so $\Delta s^2=600^2-900^2=-450{,}000\,\mathrm{m^2}$ is spacelike. Setting $\Delta t'=\gamma(\Delta t-v\Delta x/c^2)=0$ gives $v=c^2\Delta t/\Delta x=2.00\times10^8\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}=(2/3)c$. Such an inertial frame exists because the separation is spacelike. The events cannot be causally connected by a signal at or below $c$.
4. $u=(0.60+0.60)c/[1+(0.60)(0.60)]=1.20c/1.36\approx0.88235c$. The result remains below $c$ despite the ordinary numerator exceeding $c$.
5. $f_{\rm obs}/f_s=\sqrt{(1-0.80)/(1+0.80)}=\sqrt{1/9}=1/3$. The reciprocal factor applies for direct approach.
6. $E=mc^2\sqrt{1+(3/4)^2}=(5/4)mc^2$. Thus $K=(1/4)mc^2$, $\gamma=5/4$, and $u=pc^2/E=(3/5)c=0.60c$. The invariant relation fixes all quantities consistently.
7. Each initial energy is $(5/3)mc^2$ and their momenta cancel. The final stationary composite has $Mc^2=2(5/3)mc^2$, so $M=(10/3)m$. The excess over $2m$ represents retained internal energy divided by $c^2$. If energy escaped as radiation, that radiation's four-momentum would need to be included and the composite mass could be smaller.
8. With $q=Ft/(mc)$ and $dt=(mc/F)dq$, $u=cq/\sqrt{1+q^2}$, so

$$x=\frac{mc^2}{F}\int_0^{Ft/(mc)}\frac{q\,dq}{\sqrt{1+q^2}}
=\frac{mc^2}{F}\left[\sqrt{1+(Ft/mc)^2}-1\right].$$

For $|Ft|\ll mc$, the bracket is approximately $\tfrac12(Ft/mc)^2$, giving $x\approx Ft^2/(2m)$. At large positive time, the velocity expression tends to $c$ from below. Momentum continues growing as $Ft$ while speed approaches its limiting value.
