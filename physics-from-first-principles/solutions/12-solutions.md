# 12. Solutions

1. $p_{\rm gauge}=800(9.81)(3.0)=23544\,\mathrm{Pa}\approx23.5\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Absolute pressure is this plus the unspecified surface pressure.
2. $v_2=(6/2)(0.40)=1.20\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. $Q=(6.0\times10^{-4})(0.40)=2.4\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3\,s^{-1}}$.
3. $DT/Dt=2+(-0.50)(3)=0.50\,\mathrm{K\,s^{-1}}$. Motion toward cooler positions offsets part of the local warming.
4. $A=2.0\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and $Y=7.0\times10^{10}\,\mathrm{Pa}$. Stress is $7.0\times10^6\,\mathrm{Pa}$, strain $10^{-4}$, and extension $10^{-4}\,\mathrm m=0.10\,\mathrm{mm}$. Linear elasticity is assumed.
5. $t=(0.0020)^2/[2(2.0\times10^{-9})]=1000\,\mathrm s$. An rms distance describes a distribution's width, not a front position. The ideal Gaussian solution has tails at all distances; microscopic finite-speed physics lies outside that ideal limit.
6. $\bar v=Q/(\pi R^2)=\Delta pR^2/(8\eta L)$, while $v(0)=\Delta pR^2/(4\eta L)=2\bar v$. No slip fixes the integration constant by requiring zero velocity at the wall. Different boundary physics changes the profile and flow rate.
7. For $T=A\sin(kx)e^{-\alpha k^2t}$, $\partial_tT=-\alpha k^2T$ and $\alpha\partial_x^2T=-\alpha k^2T$. Thus the equation holds. The decay time is $1/(\alpha k^2)$. Doubling $k$ divides the time by four. For fixed-zero ends and the lowest mode, $k=\pi/L$.
8. Ideal Bernoulli contains pressure, kinetic, and gravitational energy per unit mass. In a uniform horizontal pipe, equal speed and height would force equal pressure within that ideal model. Real viscosity converts organized mechanical energy into thermal energy, so a maintained pressure gradient is required. An energy-loss term or viscous momentum balance is needed.
