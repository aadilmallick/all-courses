# 06. Hints

1. Use the angle between the position vector and force, not an angle to a different reference line.
2. Directly integrate $x^2(M/L)dx$ from 0 to $L$. For the second route, the two axes are separated by $L/2$.
3. First find the disk's central inertia and angular acceleration. Check work using both torque times angle and kinetic energy.
4. Substitute the appropriate inertia into the rolling-energy formula. Compare equal drops, not equal speeds.
5. Translation, torque, and the no-slip constraint determine $f$. Then compare it with $\mu_sN$.
6. Angular momentum is conserved; mechanical kinetic energy of the body's rotation need not remain unchanged when muscles do work internally.
7. Use $r^2\dot\theta$ constant, then form $r\dot\theta$ for tangential speed.
8. Keep only terms linear in the two small components; changes in $\omega_2$ are second order. Differentiate the first linearized equation and insert the third.
