# 15. Solutions

1. Magnitude is $8.99\times10^9(10^{-9})/0.20^2\approx225\,\mathrm{N\,C^{-1}}$, directed toward the negative charge.
2. Each source is distance $\sqrt{a^2+y^2}$ away. Its vertical field is $k_eq/(a^2+y^2)$ times $y/\sqrt{a^2+y^2}$. Add two copies: $E_y=2k_eqy/(a^2+y^2)^{3/2}$. For $y\gg a>0$, this tends to $2k_eq/y^2$.
3. $\mathbf E=(-Ay,-Ax,0)$. Divergence is $0+0+0=0$. Its curl is zero because the potentially nonzero component is $\partial_x(-Ax)-\partial_y(-Ay)=-A-(-A)=0$. Thus $\rho=0$ locally, despite a spatially varying nonzero field.
4. Inside, $E=Qr/(4\pi\epsilon_0R^3)$ outward for $Q>0$; outside, $E=Q/(4\pi\epsilon_0r^2)$. The field vanishes at the centre and matches at the surface. Negative $Q$ reverses direction.
5. A constant field has equal normal components relative to fixed coordinate directions on opposite faces, while outward normals reverse. Their fluxes cancel pairwise. Gauss' law therefore gives zero enclosed net charge, not zero field.
6. Divergence is $b$, while curl is $(0,0,2a)$. The $a$ terms create circulation and no local net outward flux. The $b$ term creates a longitudinal outward-flux density and no curl.
7. Between the sheets $E=\sigma/\epsilon_0\approx226\,\mathrm{N\,C^{-1}}$. Potential difference magnitude is $226(0.010)\approx2.26\,\mathrm V$. The field points from positive to negative sheet; potential decreases along it.
8. Smooth-field use of the divergence theorem across the source is invalid. Excluding a tiny sphere adds an inner boundary whose flux accounts for $q/\epsilon_0$. Equivalently, the complete distributional divergence contains $q\delta^3(\mathbf r)/\epsilon_0$, whose volume integral is nonzero when the charge is enclosed.
