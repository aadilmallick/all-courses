# 13. Solutions

1. $Q=-80\,\mathrm J$, $W=-140\,\mathrm J$, so $\Delta U=Q-W=60\,\mathrm J$.
2. $\ln a=\ln[(a/b)b]=\ln(a/b)+\ln b$, hence the result for $a,b>0$. A reference scale converts a measured dimensional quantity into a dimensionless ratio; changing units must not change a physical entropy or work prediction.
3. $W=0.50(8.314)(400)\ln3\approx1827\,\mathrm J$. The gas absorbs the same heat, since $\Delta U=0$ for an isothermal ideal gas.
4. $T_f/T_i=(V_i/V_f)^{2/3}=8^{-2/3}=1/4$.
5. $\eta_{\max}=1-300/500=0.40$. Thus $W_{\max}=480\,\mathrm J$ and $Q_{c,\min}=720\,\mathrm J$. Entropy balance verifies $-1200/500+720/300=0$ for the reversible limit.
6. $Q=0$ by insulation and $W=0$ for expansion against vacuum. Therefore $\Delta U=0$ and $\Delta T=0$ for an ideal gas. The entropy increase is $R\ln2\approx5.76\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}$. Adiabatic does not imply isentropic when the process is irreversible.
7. Equal heat capacities give $T_f=(300+500)/2=400\,\mathrm K$. Entropy change is $200\ln(400/300)+200\ln(400/500)=200\ln(16/15)\approx12.91\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}$.
8. $G_T=-S$ and $G_p=V$. If $G$ has continuous mixed second derivatives, $G_{Tp}=G_{pT}$, so $-S_p=V_T$ with the corresponding held-fixed variables. Across a non-smooth phase boundary the smooth single-phase derivation needs care.
