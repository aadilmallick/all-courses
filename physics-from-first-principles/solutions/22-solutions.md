# 22. Worked solutions

1. The exact lower energy is $(1-\sqrt{1.16})\Delta/2\approx-0.0385165\Delta$. Second order gives $-0.04\Delta$. The absolute error divided by the exact magnitude is about $0.0385$, or $3.85$ percent.
2. $H=p^2/(2m)+m\omega^2[x+F/(m\omega^2)]^2/2-F^2/(2m\omega^2)$. Every level has shift $-F^2/(2m\omega^2)$, with no linear term. The state center moves even though the first-order energy shift vanishes.
3. Stationarity gives $\alpha^3-\alpha-0.6=0$. The relevant positive minimum is approximately $\alpha=1.2212$; the energy is approximately $0.5603$ in these units. The second derivative is positive there. This is a variational upper bound, not an exact energy. The quartic confinement favors a narrower wavefunction, hence $\alpha>1$.
4. The weak-transition expression is $P\approx|v|^2t^2/\hbar^2$. It requires $|v|t/\hbar\ll1$, so breakdown occurs on the scale $t\sim\hbar/|v|$. Exact resonant evolution gives $P=\sin^2(|v|t/\hbar)$, agreeing at short time and remaining bounded.
5. $d\sigma/d\Omega=(m^2V_0^2\pi a^6/4\hbar^4)e^{-a^2q^2/2}$. For fixed $k$, increasing $a$ concentrates the normalized angular shape nearer the forward direction, since a smaller angle is needed to make $a q$ appreciable. Overall magnitude also changes; angular width and total strength are separate features.
6. The norm squared is $2+2|s|^2$. Divide by $\sqrt{2(1+|s|^2)}$. If $a=b$, the numerator is $2a(1)a(2)$, denominator $2$, giving the normalized product. The antisymmetric combination instead has norm squared $2-2|s|^2$, vanishing when the states coincide.
7. A product would require $ac=bd=0$, but $ad$ and $bc$ both nonzero, impossible. The density matrix has diagonal weights one half in 01 and 10 and negative cross coherences. Partial trace gives $I/2$ for either subsystem.
8. For the Bell state, the only outcomes are ++ and --, so the matching probability is one. In the classical mixture, each product preparation produces all four $x$-basis outcomes with equal probability one quarter; mixing them leaves the same distribution, so matching probability is one half. These predictions distinguish coherence from mere matching labels in the original basis.
