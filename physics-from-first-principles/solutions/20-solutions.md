# 20. Worked solutions

1. Normalization requires $|A|^2L^3/3=1$, so a real positive choice is $A=\sqrt3/L^{3/2}$. The left-half probability is $3L^{-3}(L/2)^3/3=1/8$. The function jumps at (L); with the ordinary free-particle kinetic Hamiltonian its derivative behavior does not lie in the usual finite-energy domain. Probability normalization and dynamical admissibility are different conditions.
2. Both energies and every fixed-level transition energy are divided by nine. The ground-state mean position moves from (L/2) to (3L/2), measured from the same left wall.
3. The endpoint expression gives $P=1/4-1/(2\pi)\approx0.09085$. Twice this plus $1/2+1/\pi$ is one.
4. $B/A=(1-1/3)/(1+1/3)=1/2$, so (R=1/4). Also (C/A=3/2), and $T=(1/3)(9/4)=3/4$. Their sum is one.
5. Value matching gives $A\sin ka=B$; slope matching gives $Ak\cos ka=-\kappa B$. Division yields $k\cot ka=-\kappa$, or $-k\cot ka=\kappa$. The exterior growing exponential would make the norm divergent as $x\to\infty$.
6. With the same (E,V_0), $T(a+1/\kappa)/T(a)\approx e^{-2}$, about (0.1353). This uses the thick-barrier leading exponential and neglects exponentially small corrections to the prefactor approximation.
7. Write $\alpha=m\omega/\hbar$. For the normalized Gaussian density, $\langle x\rangle=0$ by parity and $\langle x^2\rangle=1/(2\alpha)$. Thus $\sigma_x^2=\hbar/(2m\omega)$. Also $\langle p\rangle=0$, and $\langle p^2\rangle=\hbar^2\int(\alpha-\alpha^2x^2)|\phi|^2dx=\hbar^2\alpha/2$. Hence $\sigma_p^2=m\hbar\omega/2$, and $\sigma_x\sigma_p=\hbar/2$, saturating the uncertainty lower bound.
8. The density is $\frac12(\phi_1^2+\phi_2^2)+\phi_1\phi_2\cos[(E_2-E_1)t/\hbar]$. The beat angular frequency is $|E_2-E_1|/\hbar$. Each eigenfunction has norm one and their inner product vanishes, so integrating the density gives one. Pointwise interference can move probability while preserving the total.
