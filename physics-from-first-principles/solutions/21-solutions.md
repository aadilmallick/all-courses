# 21. Worked solutions

1. $\int|Y_1^0|^2d\Omega=(3/4\pi)(2\pi)\int_0^\pi\cos^2\theta\sin\theta d\theta=(3/2)\int_{-1}^1u^2du=1$.
2. The component eigenvalues are $-3\hbar/2,-\hbar/2,\hbar/2,3\hbar/2$. The magnitude is $\hbar\sqrt{(3/2)(5/2)}=\sqrt{15}\hbar/2$.
3. $J_+|1,0\rangle=\sqrt2\hbar|1,1\rangle$. At $m=1$, the coefficient is $\hbar\sqrt{2-2}=0$.
4. The $z$ probabilities are $3/4$ and $1/4$. The $+x$ amplitude is $(\sqrt3+1)/(2\sqrt2)$, giving probability $(2+\sqrt3)/4\approx0.9330$. Relative phase matters; changing the sign of the second amplitude would give a different $x$ probability without changing the $z$ probabilities.
5. $\langle r\rangle=(4/a_0^3)\int_0^\infty r^3e^{-2r/a_0}dr=(4/a_0^3)\,3!/(2/a_0)^4=3a_0/2$. The derivative of $r^2e^{-2r/a_0}$ is $2re^{-2r/a_0}(1-r/a_0)$; its nonzero maximum is at $a_0$.
6. $\Delta E=13.6(1-1/4)=10.2\,\mathrm{eV}$, so $\lambda=1240/10.2\approx121.6\,\mathrm{nm}$. It is ultraviolet in this leading-order model.
7. Both product terms have $m_1+m_2=0$, so $J_z$ annihilates each. Raising $|+-\rangle$ gives $\hbar|++\rangle$, and raising $|-+\rangle$ gives the same; the singlet minus sign cancels them. A triplet $|1,0\rangle$ also has $J_z=0$, so that component alone cannot distinguish total $j$.
8. The characteristic radius is $a_0/Z$, and energies are $Z^2E_n$. A typical velocity scale is $Z\alpha c$, where $\alpha=C/(\hbar c)\approx1/137$. The nonrelativistic treatment requires $Z\alpha\ll1$, with finite nuclear size and other corrections also becoming important for sufficiently heavy ions.
