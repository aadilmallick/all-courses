# 04. Complete solutions

1. With right positive, $a=-6.0/3.0=-2.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$. Set $v=5.0-2.0t=0$ to obtain $t=2.5\,\mathrm s$. The displacement is $5.0(2.5)+\tfrac12(-2.0)(2.5)^2=6.25\,\mathrm m$. At the stop the same leftward force remains, so acceleration is still $-2.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$; the cart begins to move left afterward.
2. $N=mg=39.24\,\mathrm N$. The maximum static friction is $0.50(39.24)=19.62\,\mathrm N$. At a $15\,\mathrm N$ push, static friction is $15\,\mathrm N$ opposite the push. Slipping begins once the applied force exceeds $19.62\,\mathrm N$ in the ideal model. While sliding under $25\,\mathrm N$, kinetic friction is $0.30(39.24)=11.772\,\mathrm N$. Thus $a=(25-11.772)/4.0=3.307\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$ along the push.
3. The equations are $T-1.5g=1.5a$ and $2.5g-T=2.5a$. Adding gives $a=(1.0/4.0)g=2.4525\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$. Therefore $T=1.5(9.81+2.4525)=18.39375\,\mathrm N$. The weights are $14.715\,\mathrm N$ and $24.525\,\mathrm N$, so tension lies between them.
4. $v-v_\infty=(v_0-v_\infty)e^{-t/\tau}=v_\infty e^{-t/\tau}$. Thus $v=v_\infty(1+e^{-t/\tau})$. Requiring the excess fraction $e^{-t/\tau}=0.1$ gives $t=\tau\ln10\approx2.3026\tau$. The exponential is positive for all finite real $t$, so the speed approaches terminal speed from above without crossing it.
5. $T=mv^2/R=0.25(9.0)/0.60=3.75\,\mathrm N$. Immediately after the cut the object moves along the tangent with speed $3.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, if other horizontal forces are absent. It does not travel outward along a radius.
6. Taking upward positive, $N-mg=ma_y$. For downward acceleration, $a_y=-2.0$, so $N=70(9.81-2.0)=546.7\,\mathrm N$. For upward acceleration, $N=70(9.81+2.0)=826.7\,\mathrm N$.
7. Write $v_{n+1}=v_n+\Delta t(g-v_n/\tau)$ and use $g=v_\infty/\tau$. Subtraction gives $v_{n+1}-v_\infty=(1-\Delta t/\tau)(v_n-v_\infty)$. With positive step size, decay requires $|1-\Delta t/\tau|<1$, or $0<\Delta t<2\tau$. A nonoscillatory approach requires $0\leq1-\Delta t/\tau<1$, or $0<\Delta t\leq\tau$. At $\Delta t=\tau$, Euler jumps to terminal velocity in one step; that is a numerical feature, not the exact continuous motion. At $\Delta t=2\tau$ the error alternates without decaying, so the endpoint is excluded.
8. With positive downhill, an uphill-moving block has negative velocity and downhill kinetic friction. Consequently $a=g\sin\theta+\mu_kg\cos\theta$ while that sliding regime persists. When the velocity reaches zero, evaluate static friction. It may hold the block; if it cannot, the block slides downhill and friction then points uphill. Extending the uphill formula through rest would be physically incorrect.
