# 18. Solutions

1. Displacement current is $2.0\,\mathrm{mA}$. Electric-flux rate is $I/\epsilon_0\approx2.26\times10^8\,\mathrm{V\,m\,s^{-1}}$ using $\epsilon_0\approx8.854\times10^{-12}\,\mathrm{F\,m^{-1}}$.
2. $\nabla\times(\nabla\times\mathbf E)=-\mu_0\epsilon_0\partial_t^2\mathbf E$. Using $\nabla\times(\nabla\times\mathbf E)=\nabla(\nabla\cdot\mathbf E)-\nabla^2\mathbf E$ and zero divergence gives $\nabla^2\mathbf E-\mu_0\epsilon_0\partial_t^2\mathbf E=0$. Thus speed is $1/\sqrt{\mu_0\epsilon_0}$.
3. Magnetic field points along $+z$, since $\hat{\mathbf y}\times\hat{\mathbf z}=\hat{\mathbf x}$. Amplitude is $E_0/c\approx2.0\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm T$.
4. $I=E_0^2/(2Z_0)=3600/754\approx4.77\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$.
5. $F=2P/c\approx4/(3.00\times10^8)=1.33\times10^{-8}\,\mathrm N$, directed along the incident beam's momentum.
6. Doubling frequency gives factor 16. Halving amplitude simultaneously adds factor 1/4, leaving net factor 4. These statements hold only while the stated source model remains valid.
7. $r=(Z_2-Z_1)/(Z_2+Z_1)=(n_1-n_2)/(n_1+n_2)$. Thus $R=[(n_1-n_2)/(n_1+n_2)]^2$. For 1 and 2, $r=-1/3$ and $R=1/9$. Transmitted energy fraction is 8/9 in this lossless setting.
8. The Poynting vector describes local energy flux, which can be steady circulation or transfer into matter rather than outgoing radiation. Radiation has far-zone fields falling as 1/r and can sustain finite outward power through arbitrarily large spheres. Near fields can store and return energy with different distance dependence. The full geometry and surface integral establish the interpretation.
