# 23. Worked solutions

1. The energy range is from $\epsilon_0-2t$ to $\epsilon_0+2t$, so width $4t$. Velocity is $(2ta/\hbar)\sin ka$, so its maximum magnitude is $2ta/\hbar$. At $k=0$, the curvature is $2ta^2$, giving $m^*=\hbar^2/(2ta^2)$.
2. There are $2N$ one-particle states. With $N$ electrons, the filling is one half. This elementary independent-band count does not rule out an interaction-driven insulating phase.
3. Using $e=1.602\times10^{-19}\,\mathrm C$ and $m_e=9.11\times10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}$, $\sigma=ne^2\tau/m^*\approx5.63\times10^6\,\mathrm{S/m}$. The electron density is half that of the chapter example, the time is twice as large, and the mass is half as large, so the answer is twice its conductivity.
4. Multiplication gives $np=N_cN_ve^{-(E_c-E_v)/(k_BT)}$. Define this as $n_i^2$. In an intrinsic sample $n=p$ by neutrality, so each equals the positive square root, $\sqrt{N_cN_v}e^{-E_g/(2k_BT)}$.
5. The original argument is $10^{12}$; the new one is $10^{14}$. Thus the voltage grows by $(k_BT/e)\ln100\approx0.119\,\mathrm V$, reaching about $0.835\,\mathrm V$ for the stated rounded thermal voltage. The approximation still requires the assumed nondegenerate and ionized-dopant regime.
6. With small $ka$, $\omega\approx2\sqrt{K/M}|ka/2|=a\sqrt{K/M}|k|$, so $c_s=a\sqrt{K/M}$. Multiplying $M$ by four halves the speed.
7. In $U\propto\int\hbar\omega^3/(e^{\hbar\omega/(k_BT)}-1)d\omega$, replacing $\omega$ by $k_BTx/\hbar$ supplies three powers of $T$ from $\omega^3$ and one from $d\omega$. Thus $U\propto T^4$ and $C=dU/dT\propto T^3$, while the remaining dimensionless integral is independent of $T$ in the low-temperature cutoff approximation.
8. Linearization gives $m\approx(zJ/k_BT)m$. The onset condition is $T_c^{\mathrm{MF}}=zJ/k_B$. The one-dimensional short-range Ising model has no spontaneous order at any positive temperature in the infinite zero-field limit, showing that mean field can fail qualitatively when fluctuations dominate.
