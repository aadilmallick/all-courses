# 16. Solutions

1. $V=0$ and $V=-E_0x$ both satisfy $\nabla^2V=0$. The first corresponds to zero potential on the entire boundary; the second corresponds to boundary values varying with $x$. Charge density alone leaves a homogeneous solution undetermined.
2. The image force is $F_z=-k_eq^2/(2a)^2=-k_eq^2/(4a^2)$. Differentiating $U=-k_eq^2/(4a)$ gives $dU/da=k_eq^2/(4a^2)$, hence $F_z=-dU/da$ agrees. The fictitious image is not an independently assembled physical charge.
3. $E_x=-(\pi V_0/L)\cos(\pi x/L)e^{-\pi y/L}$ and $E_y=(\pi V_0/L)\sin(\pi x/L)e^{-\pi y/L}$. Second $x$ derivative of $V$ is $-(\pi/L)^2V$, while second $y$ derivative is $+(\pi/L)^2V$, so their sum vanishes.
4. Choose the origin halfway between charges: $\mathbf p=q(\mathbf d/2)+(-q)(-\mathbf d/2)=q\mathbf d$. Far away, axial magnitude is $2k_ep/r^3$ and equatorial magnitude is $k_ep/r^3$, so the axial magnitude is twice as large. Directions differ too.
5. $C=400\,\mathrm{pF}$. Initial charge is $Q=C_0V_0=2.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ and remains fixed. Final voltage is $5.0\,\mathrm V$. Stored energy is one-quarter of initial energy because $U\propto1/C$ at fixed $Q$.
6. Final capacitance is again $400\,\mathrm{pF}$, final charge is $8.0\,\mathrm{nC}$, and stored capacitor energy is four times its initial value. The battery transfers additional charge and energy. Mechanical work and battery energy must both be included in a complete insertion calculation.
7. $\rho_b=-\nabla\cdot\mathbf P=0$ in the uniform interior. Top surface with outward normal $+\hat{\mathbf z}$ has $\sigma_b=+P_0$; bottom has $-P_0$. Side faces parallel to $\mathbf P$ have zero bound surface charge.
8. Two candidate solutions differ by harmonic $u$ with zero boundary values. Integration gives $\int|\nabla u|^2dV=0$, forcing $\nabla u=0$, then $u=0$ from the boundary. This rules out two different valid solutions but does not construct one or prove arbitrary boundary data are consistent.
