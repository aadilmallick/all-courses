# 09. Complete solutions

1. $\sin\theta_2=\sin45^\circ/1.40\approx0.50508$, so $\theta_2\approx30.34^\circ$. The wavelength is $560/1.40=400\,\mathrm{nm}$. The frequency is unchanged in this stationary linear-interface model.
2. For $s=0.60\,\mathrm m$, $1/s'=5-1/0.60=3.333\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$, so $s'=0.30\,\mathrm m$ and $M=-0.50$: real, inverted, reduced. For $s=0.10\,\mathrm m$, $1/s'=5-10=-5\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$, so $s'=-0.20\,\mathrm m$ and $M=+2$: virtual, upright, enlarged.
3. $\Delta y=\lambda D/d=(500\times10^{-9})(1.5)/(0.30\times10^{-3})=2.50\,\mathrm{mm}$. The first envelope zero has $a\sin\theta=\lambda$, while an interference maximum has $d\sin\theta=m\lambda$. Combining gives $m=d/a=5$. Thus the fifth bright order on either side is suppressed by the slit envelope in the ideal pattern.
4. $I=I_1+I_2+2\sqrt{I_1I_2}\cos\delta$. At $\cos\delta=-1$, it is $(\sqrt{I_1}-\sqrt{I_2})^2$; at $+1$, it is $(\sqrt{I_1}+\sqrt{I_2})^2$. For 9 and 4, the extrema are 25 and 1. Visibility is $(25-1)/(25+1)=24/26=12/13\approx0.9231$. Perfect coherence alone does not force a zero minimum when the amplitudes differ.
5. Relative angles are $30^\circ$ and $60^\circ$. The fraction is $(1/2)\cos^230^\circ\cos^260^\circ=(1/2)(3/4)(1/4)=3/32=0.09375$. Using $90^\circ$ directly against the first polarizer would ignore that the middle polarizer has prepared a new polarization direction.
6. Normal-incidence $R=[(1.60-1)/(1.60+1)]^2=(0.60/2.60)^2\approx0.05325$, or $5.33\%$. For light traveling from the index-1.60 medium toward air, $\theta_c=\arcsin(1/1.60)\approx38.68^\circ$. The air-to-medium direction does not have a real critical angle for total internal reflection in this ideal two-dielectric configuration.
7. Each cycle corresponds to $\lambda_0/2=300\,\mathrm{nm}$ mirror motion. Thus $\Delta L=240(300\,\mathrm{nm})=72{,}000\,\mathrm{nm}=72\,\mu\mathrm m$. The arm includes the trip to the mirror and the return trip, both lengthened by the displacement.
8. $n_f=\sqrt{1(2.25)}=1.50$. Set $n_ft=\lambda_0/4$, giving $t=600/(4\cdot1.50)=100\,\mathrm{nm}$. Ideal cancellation is designed for a chosen wavelength and incidence angle with lossless appropriate-index media. Dispersion, absorption, thickness errors, polarization at oblique incidence, and a broadband source limit the cancellation away from those conditions.
