# 04. Hints

1. Keep right positive, so the force and acceleration are negative. Use velocity to locate the turning time before substituting into position.
2. Compare the demanded static friction with its maximum before choosing a friction regime. Once sliding starts, use the kinetic coefficient.
3. Use upward positive for the lighter mass and downward positive for the heavier. Add the equations to eliminate tension.
4. Subtract the terminal velocity. The remaining difference obeys exponential decay, even when it begins positive.
5. The acceleration is inward even though speed is constant. After the cut, use the velocity direction just before the cut.
6. The scale force is the normal force. Write the signed vertical acceleration in an upward-positive equation.
7. Subtract $v_\infty$ from both sides of $v_{n+1}=v_n+(g-v_n/\tau)\Delta t$. Decay requires the absolute value of the multiplier to be less than one; monotonicity also constrains its sign.
8. Friction opposes sliding relative to the plane. The sign of velocity, rather than the sign of acceleration, chooses the friction direction.
