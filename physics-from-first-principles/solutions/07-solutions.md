# 07. Complete solutions

1. $\omega_0=\sqrt{20/0.20}=10\,\mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$. Thus $x(t)=0.030\cos10t+0.040\sin10t$ in meters. Amplitude is $\sqrt{0.030^2+0.040^2}=0.050\,\mathrm m$. Energy is $\tfrac12kC^2=0.025\,\mathrm J$; directly, the initial terms are $0.009\,\mathrm J$ spring energy and $0.016\,\mathrm J$ kinetic energy, with the same sum.
2. $I=ML^2/3$ and torque is $-Mg(L/2)\sin\theta$. Replacing $\sin\theta$ by $\theta$ gives $\ddot\theta+[3g/(2L)]\theta=0$, so $T=2\pi\sqrt{2L/(3g)}$. The rod is modeled as rigid and uniform with an ideal pivot; the additional linearization requires small angles in radians. Neglect of air resistance and pivot friction is another physical approximation.
3. $b_c=2\sqrt{mk}=10\,\mathrm{kg\,s^{-1}}$. Thus $b=2$ is underdamped, $b=10$ critically damped, and $b=14$ overdamped. “Critical” refers to the repeated characteristic root, not the absence of every possible crossing of equilibrium under arbitrary initial conditions.
4. $(2+3i)(1-2i)=2-4i+3i-6i^2=8-i$. $|3-4i|=5$. $1/(1-i)=(1+i)/2$. Multiplying by $i$ maps $(a,b)$ to $(-b,a)$, a positive quarter-turn in the complex plane.
5. $\omega_0=10\,\mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$. At that drive frequency, $A=F_0/(b\omega_0)=0.20\,\mathrm m$ and $\delta=\pi/2$. Velocity amplitude is $A\Omega=2.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. Hence $\langle P\rangle=\tfrac12b(A\Omega)^2=2.0\,\mathrm W$. The steady displacement can be written $0.20\sin10t$; initial conditions may require an additional decaying transient.
6. Write $C=F_0/(2m\omega_0)$. Then $\dot x=C\sin\omega_0t+C\omega_0t\cos\omega_0t$ and $\ddot x=2C\omega_0\cos\omega_0t-C\omega_0^2t\sin\omega_0t$. Adding $\omega_0^2x$ leaves $2C\omega_0\cos\omega_0t=(F_0/m)\cos\omega_0t$. A bounded pure sinusoid at the natural frequency is annihilated by the left-hand homogeneous operator, so it cannot equal a nonzero resonant force.
7. Adding gives $m(\ddot x_1+\ddot x_2)=-k(x_1+x_2)$, so $\omega_+=\sqrt{k/m}$. Subtracting gives $m(\ddot x_1-\ddot x_2)=-(k+2\kappa)(x_1-x_2)$, so $\omega_-=\sqrt{(k+2\kappa)/m}$. When $x_1=x_2$, the middle spring's extension is zero at every instant, explaining why its stiffness cannot affect that mode.
8. With constant force $F_0$, $x(t)=\int_0^t[F_0/(m\omega_0)]\sin[\omega_0(t-t')]dt'$. Integrating gives $x=F_0[1-\cos\omega_0t]/(m\omega_0^2)=(F_0/k)(1-\cos\omega_0t)$. The new static equilibrium is $F_0/k$, but the sudden switch supplies an initial mismatch, so without damping the mass oscillates around that equilibrium with amplitude $F_0/k$. Slow application or nonzero damping would change the transient.
