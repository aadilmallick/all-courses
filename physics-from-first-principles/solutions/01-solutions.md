# Chapter 01 worked solutions

1. $72\,\mathrm{km/h}\times(1000\,\mathrm{m/km})\times(1\,\mathrm{h}/3600\,\mathrm{s})=20\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Both km and h cancel; metres and seconds remain.
2. The sides are 0.30 m and 0.20 m, so area is 0.060 m². In centimetres it is $30\times20=600$ cm². Since 1 cm² is $10^{-4}$ m², $600\times10^{-4}=0.060$ m², a separate conversion check.
3. Displacement is $0.8-(-1.2)=2.0$ m. Average velocity is $2.0/0.50=4.0$ m/s toward increasing coordinate. Distance is at least 2.0 m, but it cannot be determined exactly without the intervening path. Moving directly gives 2.0 m; making a detour gives more while preserving the endpoints.
4. Subtracting $D_0$ yields $D-D_0=vt$. For $v\ne0$, $t=(D-D_0)/v$. If $v=0$ and $D\ne D_0$, no time satisfies the equation. If $v=0$ and $D=D_0$, every time in the model's domain satisfies it. A negative algebraic time must also be checked against the physical time domain.
5. $V=15\times10^{-6}$ m³. Therefore $\rho=0.120/(15\times10^{-6})=8000$ kg/m³. The factor $10^{-6}$ comes from cubing $10^{-2}$, not from a linear conversion.
6. $[m/k]=\mathrm{kg}/(\mathrm{kg/s^2})=\mathrm{s^2}$, so its square root has unit s. The factor $2\pi$ is dimensionless. This passes the dimensional test but does not prove the numerical factor or physical assumptions; a derivation of harmonic motion is still required.
7. Multiply by 1.20 and then 0.80: the final factor is 0.96. The net change is a 4% decrease. The second change subtracts 20% of the increased value, which is 24% of the original value.
8. $b=(0.80-0.20)/(4-1)=0.20$ m/s. Then $a=0.20-(0.20)(1)=0$ m, so $x(2.5\,\mathrm{s})=0.50$ m. Extrapolation to 100 s requires continued constant velocity in the same physical regime, with no collision, endpoint or change of driving conditions. The data alone do not establish that assumption.
