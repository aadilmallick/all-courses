# 11. Complete solutions

1. $L_{\dot x}=m\dot x$ and $L_x=-kx$, so $m\ddot x+kx=0$. With $p=m\dot x$, $H=\dot xp-L=\tfrac12m\dot x^2+\tfrac12kx^2$. It is conserved because the Lagrangian has no explicit time dependence. Direct differentiation gives $\dot H=\dot x(m\ddot x+kx)=0$.
2. $x=\ell\sin\theta$, $y=-\ell\cos\theta$ give $v^2=\ell^2\dot\theta^2$. Thus $L=\tfrac12m\ell^2\dot\theta^2-mg\ell(1-\cos\theta)$. Euler–Lagrange gives $m\ell^2\ddot\theta+mg\ell\sin\theta=0$. This equation is exact for the ideal point-mass fixed-length model; only the subsequent replacement $\sin\theta\approx\theta$ invokes a small angle. Ideal rigidity, negligible drag and a fixed frictionless pivot are separate physical assumptions.
3. $S'-S=\int_{t_i}^{t_f}dF/dt\,dt=F(q_f,t_f)-F(q_i,t_i)$. Under fixed endpoint coordinates and times this difference is constant across allowed varied paths, so its first variation is zero. Therefore $\delta S'=\delta S$ and both select the same stationary paths. If endpoint conditions change, the boundary contribution must be reconsidered rather than discarded automatically.
4. $\dot q=p/m$ and $\dot p=-4\alpha q^3$. Hence $\dot H=(p/m)(-4\alpha q^3)+(4\alpha q^3)(p/m)=0$. The bracket is $\{q^2,p^2\}=(2q)(2p)-0=4qp$.
5. $r_p=a(1-e)=0.50a$ and $r_a=a(1+e)=1.50a$. At these smooth radial extrema, $\dot r=0$, so velocity is tangential. Conserved $\ell=\mu rv$ gives $v_p/v_a=r_a/r_p=3$. Eccentricity changes speeds around the orbit without changing the period for a fixed semimajor axis and fixed total gravitational mass.
6. $T^2=4\pi^2\mu a^3/\kappa$, with $\mu=Mm/(M+m)$ and $\kappa=GMm$, becomes $T^2=4\pi^2a^3/[G(M+m)]$. If $M\gg m$, $M+m\approx M$, giving the earlier fixed-center result. Here $a$ describes the relative separation ellipse; each body's orbit about the center of mass has a different semimajor axis.
7. Forward Euler for the oscillator is $q'=q+hp/m$, $p'=p-hkq$, where $h=\Delta t$. The derivative array has entries $(1,h/m;-hk,1)$ and determinant $1+h^2k/m=1+h^2\omega_0^2>1$. Symplectic Euler has entries $(1-h^2k/m,h/m;-hk,1)$ and determinant one. An area-preserving map can rotate at the wrong frequency or trace a deformed energy curve, so preserving area alone does not establish the desired trajectory. Step refinement and comparison with known solutions remain necessary.
8. With error $\epsilon(t)\approx\epsilon_0e^{\lambda t}$ up to a fixed tolerance, the horizon is $t=\lambda^{-1}\ln(\epsilon_{\rm tol}/\epsilon_0)$. Replacing $\epsilon_0$ by $\epsilon_0/100$ extends it by $\Delta t=\ln100/0.20\approx23.03\,\mathrm s$. This assumes the same effective exponential growth rate over the relevant range. Saturation, changing local rates, model error, or nonchaotic regimes can invalidate that simple estimate.
